    Formal MDP Document: Consumption-Labor-Saving Model with Intensive Margin¶

    1. Rosetta Stone¶

    HARK variable DDSL concept Mathematical notation
    kNrm Prestate/Arrival perch state \(k_t\)
    pLvlPrev Prestate/Arrival perch state \(p_{t-1}\)
    PermShk Arrival shock \(\psi_t\)
    TranShk Arrival shock \(\theta_t\)
    pLvl Decision perch state \(p_t\)
    bNrm Decision perch state \(b_t\)
    cNrm Control/Action \(c_t\)
    Lbr Control/Action \(\ell_t\)
    aNrm Continuation perch state \(a_t\)
    mNrm Market resources (derived) \(m_t\)
    yNrm Labor income (derived) \(y_t\)
    live Survival shock \(\mathbb{1}_{\text{survive}}\)

    Information Set Hierarchy: - Arrival perch: Agent knows \((k_t, p_{t-1})\) but not yet \((\psi_t, \theta_t)\) - Decision perch: Agent observes \((b_t, p_t, \theta_t)\) after shocks realize - Continuation perch: Agent has chosen \((c_t, \ell_t)\) and arrives at \(a_t\)

    2. Model¶

    2.1 State Spaces¶

    The model features a two-dimensional continuous state space at the arrival perch:

    • Normalized capital holdings: \(k_t \in \mathbb{R}_+\) represents beginning-of-period assets normalized by last period's permanent income \(p_{t-1}\)
    • Permanent income level: \(p_{t-1} \in \mathbb{R}_{++}\) represents the agent's permanent income level entering period \(t\)

    At the decision perch, the state expands to include the realized transitory shock: - Normalized bank balances: \(b_t \in \mathbb{R}_+\) - Permanent income level: \(p_t \in \mathbb{R}_{++}\) - Transitory productivity: \(\theta_t \in \mathbb{R}_+\)

    2.2 Action Space¶

    The agent chooses two control variables:

    • Normalized consumption: \(c_t \in [0, m_t]\) where \(m_t \coloneqq b_t + y_t\) represents normalized market resources
    • Labor supply: \(\ell_t \in [0, 1]\) represents the fraction of time allocated to work (intensive margin)

    The agent simultaneously determines consumption and labor supply to maximize expected discounted utility subject to the budget constraint.

    2.3 Shock Distributions¶

    Joint income shock distribution: The pair \((\psi_t, \theta_t)\) of permanent and transitory productivity shocks follows the joint distribution \(F_{\text{Inc}}(\psi, \theta)\) specified by IncShkDstn. We write: $\((\psi_t, \theta_t) \sim F_{\text{Inc}}\)$

    These shocks realize at the beginning of period \(t\), after the agent arrives with state \((k_t, p_{t-1})\) but before consumption and labor decisions.

    Survival shock: At the end of the period, the agent survives with probability \(\pi \in [0,1]\) (parameter LivPrb): $\(\mathbb{1}_{\text{survive}} \sim \text{Bernoulli}(\pi)\)$

    Non-survivors exit the model and may be replaced by newborns.

    2.4 Transition Dynamics¶

    The permanent income evolution follows: $\(p_t = p_{t-1} \cdot \Gamma \cdot \psi_t\)$ where \(\Gamma\) (PermGroFac) is the deterministic growth factor and \(\psi_t\) is the permanent shock.

    Normalized bank balances reflect returns on previous assets, scaled by income growth: $\(b_t = \frac{R \cdot k_t}{\Gamma \cdot \psi_t}\)$ where \(R\) (Rfree) is the gross risk-free interest rate.

    Labor income is generated by combining transitory productivity with chosen labor supply: $\(y_t = W \cdot \theta_t \cdot \ell_t\)$ where \(W\) (WageRte) is the wage rate and \(\theta_t \cdot \ell_t\) represents effective labor supply.

    Market resources combine bank balances and labor income: $\(m_t = b_t + y_t = b_t + W \cdot \theta_t \cdot \ell_t\)$

    End-of-period assets satisfy the budget constraint: $\(a_t = m_t - c_t = b_t + W \cdot \theta_t \cdot \ell_t - c_t\)$

    Next period's normalized capital for survivors: $\(k_{t+1} = a_t\)$ Note that \(k_{t+1}\) is already normalized by \(p_t\), which becomes next period's \(p_{t-1}\).

    2.5 Initial Distribution¶

    At model birth (initialization), agents draw: - Initial permanent income: \(p_0 \sim F_{\text{pLvl}}\) (pLvlInitDstn) - Initial normalized capital: \(k_0 \sim F_{\text{kNrm}}\) (kNrmInitDstn)

    3. Bellman Equation¶

    The agent's problem decomposes into three value functions corresponding to the ADC (Arrival-Decision-Continuation) perches.

    3.1 Arrival Value Function¶

    At the arrival perch, the agent has not yet observed income shocks. The arrival value represents the expected value before shocks:

    \[\mathcal{V}^A(k_t, p_{t-1}) = \mathbb{E}_{(\psi_t, \theta_t) \sim F_{\text{Inc}}}\left[\mathcal{V}^D(b_t, p_t, \theta_t)\right]\]

    where the decision perch state variables are determined by: $\(\begin{aligned} p_t &= p_{t-1} \cdot \Gamma \cdot \psi_t \\ b_t &= \frac{R \cdot k_t}{\Gamma \cdot \psi_t} \end{aligned}\)$

    Information set at arrival: \(\mathcal{F}^A_t = \{k_t, p_{t-1}\}\)

    3.2 Decision Value Function¶

    At the decision perch, shocks have realized and the agent chooses consumption and labor supply:

    \[\mathcal{V}^D(b_t, p_t, \theta_t) = \max_{c_t, \ell_t} \left\{ u(c_t \cdot p_t, 1 - \ell_t) + \beta \pi \cdot \mathcal{V}^C(a_t, p_t) \right\}\]

    subject to: $\(\begin{aligned} y_t &= W \cdot \theta_t \cdot \ell_t \\ m_t &= b_t + y_t \\ a_t &= m_t - c_t \\ c_t &\geq 0, \quad \ell_t \in [0, 1], \quad a_t \geq 0 \end{aligned}\)$

    Here \(u(\cdot, \cdot)\) is the period utility function over consumption in levels (\(c_t \cdot p_t\)) and leisure (\(1 - \ell_t\)), and \(\beta\) is the time discount factor.

    Information set at decision: \(\mathcal{F}^D_t = \{b_t, p_t, \theta_t\}\)

    Note on survival: The factor \(\pi\) represents the continuation probability. Non-survivors receive no continuation value, implicitly setting their terminal value to zero.

    3.3 Continuation Value Function¶

    At the continuation perch, decisions have been made and assets \(a_t\) are carried forward:

    \[\mathcal{V}^C(a_t, p_t) = \mathcal{V}^A(k_{t+1}, p_t)\]

    where the twist mapping determines next period's arrival state: $\(k_{t+1} = a_t, \quad p_{t} \text{ becomes } p_{t}\)$

    This value represents the ex-post value of having chosen to save \(a_t\), normalized by permanent income \(p_t\), before next period's shocks.

    Information set at continuation: \(\mathcal{F}^C_t = \{a_t, p_t\}\)

    3.4 Complete Recursive Structure¶

    Combining the three perches yields the complete Bellman operator:

    \[\begin{aligned} \mathcal{V}^A(k_t, p_{t-1}) &= \mathbb{E}_{(\psi_t, \theta_t)}\left[\mathcal{V}^D\left(\frac{R \cdot k_t}{\Gamma \cdot \psi_t}, p_{t-1} \cdot \Gamma \cdot \psi_t, \theta_t\right)\right] \\ \mathcal{V}^D(b_t, p_t, \theta_t) &= \max_{c_t, \ell_t} \left\{ u(c_t \cdot p_t, 1 - \ell_t) + \beta \pi \cdot \mathcal{V}^A(b_t + W \cdot \theta_t \cdot \ell_t - c_t, p_t) \right\} \\ \end{aligned}\]

    4. First-Order Conditions¶

    4.1 Euler Equations¶

    The optimal choices of consumption and labor satisfy the first-order conditions with respect to each control.

    Consumption FOC: Taking the derivative with respect to \(c_t\):

    \[u_c(c_t \cdot p_t, 1 - \ell_t) \cdot p_t = \beta \pi \cdot \mathcal{V}^C_a(a_t, p_t)\]

    where \(u_c\) denotes the marginal utility of consumption.

    Labor supply FOC: Taking the derivative with respect to \(\ell_t\):

    \[-u_\ell(c_t \cdot p_t, 1 - \ell_t) = W \cdot \theta_t \cdot \beta \pi \cdot \mathcal{V}^C_a(a_t, p_t)\]

    where \(u_\ell\) denotes the marginal utility of leisure (negative of marginal disutility of labor).

    Combining FOCs: The labor-consumption tradeoff satisfies:

    \[\frac{u_\ell(c_t \cdot p_t, 1 - \ell_t)}{u_c(c_t \cdot p_t, 1 - \ell_t)} = W \cdot \theta_t \cdot p_t\]

    This is the intratemporal condition: the marginal rate of substitution between leisure and consumption equals the real wage (wage rate times transitory productivity).

    4.2 Envelope Condition¶

    The marginal value of assets at the continuation perch satisfies:

    \[\mathcal{V}^C_a(a_t, p_t) = \mathbb{E}_{(\psi_{t+1}, \theta_{t+1})}\left[\mathcal{V}^D_b(b_{t+1}, p_{t+1}, \theta_{t+1}) \cdot \frac{R}{\Gamma \cdot \psi_{t+1}}\right]\]

    This follows from differentiating the arrival value with respect to \(k_{t+1} = a_t\) and applying the chain rule through \(b_{t+1} = R \cdot k_{t+1} / (\Gamma \cdot \psi_{t+1})\).

    By the envelope theorem at the decision stage: $\(\mathcal{V}^D_b(b_{t+1}, p_{t+1}, \theta_{t+1}) = u_c(c_{t+1} \cdot p_{t+1}, 1 - \ell_{t+1}) \cdot p_{t+1}\)$

    4.3 Intertemporal Euler Equation¶

    Combining the consumption FOC with the envelope condition:

    \[u_c(c_t \cdot p_t, 1 - \ell_t) \cdot p_t = \beta \pi \cdot R \cdot \mathbb{E}_{(\psi_{t+1}, \theta_{t+1})}\left[\frac{u_c(c_{t+1} \cdot p_{t+1}, 1 - \ell_{t+1}) \cdot p_{t+1}}{\Gamma \cdot \psi_{t+1}}\right]\]

    Since \(p_{t+1} = p_t \cdot \Gamma \cdot \psi_{t+1}\), this simplifies to:

    \[u_c(c_t \cdot p_t, 1 - \ell_t) = \beta \pi \cdot R \cdot \mathbb{E}_{(\psi_{t+1}, \theta_{t+1})}\left[u_c(c_{t+1} \cdot p_{t+1}, 1 - \ell_{t+1})\right]\]

    This is the standard Euler equation relating current marginal utility to expected future marginal utility, adjusted for interest, survival, and discounting.

    4.4 Policy Functions¶

    The solution to the Bellman equation yields policy functions:

    \[\begin{aligned} c_t &= c^*(b_t, \theta_t) \quad \text{(`cFunc`)} \\ \ell_t &= \ell^*(b_t, \theta_t) \quad \text{(`LbrFunc`)} \end{aligned}\]

    These functions map the decision perch state \((b_t, \theta_t)\) to optimal choices. Note that permanent income \(p_t\) does not appear due to homotheticity assumptions typical in consumption-saving models. The normalization by permanent income ensures policy functions depend only on normalized states.

    4.5 Endogenous Grid Method (EGM) Representation¶

    For consumption-saving models, the endogenous gridpoint method can efficiently solve for policy functions by:

    1. Constructing a grid over end-of-period assets \(\{a_i\}\)
    2. Computing continuation values and marginal values on this grid
    3. Using the Euler equation to find consumption: \(c_i = (u_c)^{-1}\left(\beta \pi \cdot \mathcal{V}^C_a(a_i, p_t) / p_t\right)\)
    4. Computing beginning-of-period resources: \(m_i = a_i + c_i\)
    5. Interpolating to obtain \(c^*(m, \theta)\)

    With labor supply, the intratemporal condition couples \(c_t\) and \(\ell_t\), requiring joint solution of both FOCs at each gridpoint.

    5. Forward Operator¶

    The distribution of agents over states evolves through the model dynamics. Let \(\Phi_t(k, p)\) denote the joint probability measure over normalized capital and permanent income at time \(t\).
