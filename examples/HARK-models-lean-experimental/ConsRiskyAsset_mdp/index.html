    Formal MDP Document: Consumption-Saving with Risky Asset Allocation¶

    Timing convention: The model operates in normalized space where all monetary variables are divided by permanent income from the previous period. The twist mapping connects end-of-period normalized assets \(a_t\) to beginning-of-next-period normalized wealth \(k_{t+1}\), and current permanent income \(p_t\) to next period's incoming permanent income \(p_t\) (which becomes \(p_{t}\) at the start of period \(t+1\)).

    2. Model¶

    2.1 State Spaces¶

    Arrival (Prestate) Space: At the beginning of period \(t\), the agent arrives with: - \(k_t \in \mathbb{R}_+\) — normalized wealth (assets from previous period divided by \(p_{t-1}\)) - \(p_{t-1} \in \mathbb{R}_{++}\) — permanent income level carried forward from previous period

    Decision Space: After observing income shocks, the agent has: - \(m_t \in \mathbb{R}_+\) — normalized market resources (cash-on-hand) - \(p_t \in \mathbb{R}_{++}\) — updated permanent income level

    Continuation Space: After making consumption and portfolio decisions but before observing asset returns: - \(w_t \in \mathbb{R}_+\) — normalized wealth after consumption - \(\varsigma_t \in [0,1]\) — portfolio share in risky assets - \(p_t \in \mathbb{R}_{++}\) — permanent income level

    Terminal Space: After asset returns realize: - \(a_t \in \mathbb{R}_+\) — normalized end-of-period assets - \(p_t \in \mathbb{R}_{++}\) — permanent income level

    2.2 Action Spaces¶

    The agent chooses: - \(c_t \in [0, m_t]\) — normalized consumption - \(\varsigma_t \in [0,1]\) — portfolio share allocated to risky assets

    2.3 Shock Distributions¶

    Income Shocks (Arrival): The joint distribution of permanent and transitory income shocks is given by $\((\psi_t, \theta_t) \sim F_{\text{Inc}}\)$ where \(\psi_t \in \mathbb{R}_{++}\) is the permanent income shock (typically mean-one) and \(\theta_t \in \mathbb{R}_+\) is the transitory income shock (also typically mean-one).

    Asset Return Shocks (Continuation): The risky asset return is drawn from $\(R_t \sim F_{\text{Risky}}\)$ where \(R_t \in \mathbb{R}_+\) is the gross return factor on the risky asset.

    Survival Shocks: At the end of each period, the agent survives with probability \(\pi \in (0,1]\): $\(\mathbb{1}_{\text{live},t} \sim \text{Bernoulli}(\pi)\)$

    2.4 Transition Equations¶

    The dynamics unfold in three stages within each period:

    Stage 1 (Arrival to Decision): Given prestate \((k_t, p_{t-1})\) and income shocks \((\psi_t, \theta_t)\):

    The permanent income growth factor is $\(G_t \coloneqq \Gamma \cdot \psi_t\)$ where \(\Gamma > 0\) is the deterministic permanent income growth factor.

    Permanent income evolves as $\(p_t \coloneqq p_{t-1} \cdot G_t = p_{t-1} \cdot \Gamma \cdot \psi_t\)$

    Normalized bank balances account for permanent income growth: $\(b_t \coloneqq \frac{k_t}{G_t}\)$

    Normalized labor income equals the transitory shock: $\(y_t \coloneqq \theta_t\)$

    Normalized market resources combine bank balances and labor income: $\(m_t \coloneqq b_t + y_t = \frac{k_t}{G_t} + \theta_t\)$

    Stage 2 (Decision to Continuation): Given decision state \(m_t\) and choices \((c_t, \varsigma_t)\):

    Normalized wealth after consumption is $\(w_t \coloneqq m_t - c_t\)$

    Stage 3 (Continuation to Terminal): Given continuation state \((w_t, \varsigma_t)\) and risky return \(R_t\):

    The realized portfolio return is $\(R_t^{\text{port}} \coloneqq R_f + (R_t - R_f) \cdot \varsigma_t\)$ where \(R_f > 0\) is the risk-free return factor.

    Normalized end-of-period assets are $\(a_t \coloneqq R_t^{\text{port}} \cdot w_t = [R_f + (R_t - R_f) \cdot \varsigma_t] \cdot w_t\)$

    Risky asset holdings are $\(q_t \coloneqq a_t \cdot \varsigma_t\)$

    Twist (Terminal to Next Arrival): The normalized assets and permanent income from period \(t\) become the arrival state for period \(t+1\): $\(k_{t+1} \coloneqq a_t, \quad p_t^{\text{incoming}} \coloneqq p_t\)$

    where \(p_t^{\text{incoming}}\) serves as \(p_t\) in the next period's dynamics (i.e., what we called \(p_{t-1}\) when indexing from period \(t+1\)'s perspective).

    2.5 Preferences¶

    The agent has time-separable CRRA preferences over consumption with discount factor \(\beta \in (0,1)\) and relative risk aversion \(\rho > 0\): $\(u(C) \coloneqq \frac{C^{1-\rho}}{1-\rho}\)$

    In normalized form, with consumption \(c_t\) measured relative to \(p_{t-1}\), actual consumption is \(C_t = c_t \cdot p_{t-1}\), giving: $\(u(c_t \cdot p_{t-1}) = \frac{(c_t \cdot p_{t-1})^{1-\rho}}{1-\rho} = p_{t-1}^{1-\rho} \cdot \frac{c_t^{1-\rho}}{1-\rho}\)$

    2.6 Initial Conditions¶

    At model birth (period 0), the agent draws: - \(p_{-1} \sim F_{p,\text{init}}\) — initial permanent income level - \(k_0 \sim F_{k,\text{init}}\) — initial normalized wealth

    3. Bellman Equation¶

    The value function is decomposed across three perches, each corresponding to a different information set within the period.

    3.1 Arrival Value Function (Pre-Income Shock)¶

    At the arrival perch, before income shocks are observed, the value function is: $\(\mathcal{V}_t^{\text{arr}}(k_t, p_{t-1}) \coloneqq \mathbb{E}_{\psi_t, \theta_t}\left[\mathcal{V}_t^{\text{dec}}(m_t, p_t) \,\big|\, k_t, p_{t-1}\right]\)$

    where the expectation is over income shocks \((\psi_t, \theta_t) \sim F_{\text{Inc}}\), and market resources are determined by: $\(m_t = \frac{k_t}{\Gamma \psi_t} + \theta_t, \quad p_t = p_{t-1} \cdot \Gamma \cdot \psi_t\)$

    Information set at arrival perch: \(\mathcal{I}_t^{\text{arr}} = \{k_t, p_{t-1}\}\)

    3.2 Decision Value Function (Post-Income, Pre-Decision)¶

    At the decision perch, after observing income shocks, the agent solves: $\(\mathcal{V}_t^{\text{dec}}(m_t, p_t) \coloneqq \max_{c_t \in [0, m_t], \,\varsigma_t \in [0,1]} \left\{p_{t-1}^{1-\rho} \cdot u(c_t) + \beta \pi \cdot \mathcal{V}_t^{\text{cont}}(w_t, \varsigma_t, p_t)\right\}\)$

    where \(w_t = m_t - c_t\) and \(p_{t-1}\) is the permanent income level used for normalization.

    Note on normalization: Since \(p_t = p_{t-1} \cdot G_t\), we have \(p_{t-1}^{1-\rho} = (p_t / G_t)^{1-\rho}\). The normalized flow utility is: $\(p_{t-1}^{1-\rho} \cdot \frac{c_t^{1-\rho}}{1-\rho} = \left(\frac{p_t}{G_t}\right)^{1-\rho} \cdot \frac{c_t^{1-\rho}}{1-\rho}\)$

    Information set at decision perch: \(\mathcal{I}_t^{\text{dec}} = \{m_t, p_t, p_{t-1}\} = \{m_t, G_t, p_{t-1}\}\)

    3.3 Continuation Value Function (Post-Decision, Pre-Return)¶

    At the continuation perch, after making consumption and portfolio choices but before observing asset returns: $\(\mathcal{V}_t^{\text{cont}}(w_t, \varsigma_t, p_t) \coloneqq \mathbb{E}_{R_t}\left[\mathcal{V}_{t+1}^{\text{arr}}(a_t, p_t) \,\big|\, w_t, \varsigma_t, p_t\right]\)$

    where the expectation is over risky returns \(R_t \sim F_{\text{Risky}}\), and end-of-period assets are: $\(a_t = [R_f + (R_t - R_f) \cdot \varsigma_t] \cdot w_t\)$

    By the twist mapping, \(a_t\) becomes \(k_{t+1}\) and \(p_t\) becomes \(p_t^{\text{incoming}}\) (the permanent income at arrival in period \(t+1\)).

    Information set at continuation perch: \(\mathcal{I}_t^{\text{cont}} = \{w_t, \varsigma_t, p_t\}\)

    3.4 Complete Recursive Formulation¶

    Combining all three perches, the complete Bellman equation is:

    \[\mathcal{V}_t^{\text{arr}}(k_t, p_{t-1}) = \mathbb{E}_{\psi_t, \theta_t}\left[\max_{c_t, \varsigma_t} \left\{p_{t-1}^{1-\rho} \cdot u(c_t) + \beta \pi \cdot \mathbb{E}_{R_t}\left[\mathcal{V}_{t+1}^{\text{arr}}(a_t, p_t)\right]\right\}\right]\]

    subject to: \begin{align} G_t &= \Gamma \cdot \psi_t\ p_t &= p_{t-1} \cdot G_t\ b_t &= k_t / G_t\ m_t &= b_t + \theta_t\ w_t &= m_t - c_t\ a_t &= [R_f + (R_t - R_f) \cdot \varsigma_t] \cdot w_t\ c_t &\in [0, m_t]\ \varsigma_t &\in [0,1] \end{align}

    3.5 Stationary Formulation¶

    For an infinite horizon problem with no explicit time dependence in primitives, we drop time subscripts and write:

    \[\mathcal{V}^{\text{arr}}(k, p) = \mathbb{E}_{\psi, \theta}\left[\max_{c, \varsigma} \left\{p^{1-\rho} \cdot u(c) + \beta \pi \cdot \mathbb{E}_{R}\left[\mathcal{V}^{\text{arr}}(a, p')\right]\right\}\right]\]

    where primes denote next-period values and: - \(m = k/(\Gamma \psi) + \theta\) - \(p' = p \cdot \Gamma \cdot \psi\) - \(w = m - c\) - \(a = [R_f + (R - R_f) \cdot \varsigma] \cdot w\)
