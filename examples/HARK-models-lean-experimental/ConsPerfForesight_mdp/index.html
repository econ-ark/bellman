    Perfect Foresight Consumption-Saving Model with Mortality Risk¶

    1. Rosetta Stone¶

    HARK variable DDSL concept Mathematical notation

    pLvlPrev Prestate / Arrival perch \(P_{t-1}\) (permanent income)
    yNrm Computed constant \(y = 1\) (normalized income)
    pLvl Decision perch state \(P_t = P_{t-1} \cdot \Gamma\)
    bNrm Decision perch state \(b_t = R k_t / \Gamma\)

    cNrm Control / Decision perch \(c_t\) (normalized consumption)

    Rfree Parameter \(R\) (gross interest rate)

    LivPrb Parameter \(\pi\) (survival probability)

    Perch Structure: - Arrival perch: Agent begins period with \((k_t, P_{t-1})\) - Decision perch: After income realization, state is \((m_t, P_t)\) - Continuation perch: After consumption choice, state is \((a_t, P_t)\), then mortality shock realizes

    2. Model¶

    2.1 Primitives¶

    The agent lives in discrete time \(t = 0, 1, 2, \ldots\) and faces mortality risk each period. The agent has CRRA preferences over consumption sequences with discount factor \(\beta\) and relative risk aversion \(\rho\).

    2.2 State Variables¶

    At the arrival perch (beginning of period \(t\)), the state is \((k_t, P_{t-1})\) where: - \(k_t \in \mathbb{R}_+\) denotes normalized capital holdings (assets carried forward from period \(t-1\), normalized by permanent income \(P_{t-1}\)) - \(P_{t-1} \in \mathbb{R}_{++}\) denotes the permanent income level from the previous period

    2.3 Within-Period Dynamics¶

    The within-period state evolution follows this sequence:

    Step 1: Permanent income growth. At the start of period \(t\), permanent income grows deterministically: $\(P_t \coloneqq P_{t-1} \cdot \Gamma\)$ where \(\Gamma > 0\) is the permanent income growth factor.

    Step 2: Bank balances. The agent receives the return on capital, normalized by the new permanent income level: $\(b_t \coloneqq \frac{R \cdot k_t}{\Gamma}\)$ where \(R > 0\) is the gross risk-free interest rate.

    Step 3: Labor income. The agent receives normalized labor income: $\(y_t \coloneqq 1\)$ This normalization implies that labor income grows at rate \(\Gamma\) in levels (i.e., level labor income is \(P_{t-1}\), which after growth becomes \(P_t\), and \(P_t / P_t = 1\)).

    Step 4: Market resources. Total normalized resources available for consumption are: $\(m_t \coloneqq b_t + y_t = \frac{R \cdot k_t}{\Gamma} + 1\)$

    2.4 Action Space¶

    At the decision perch, given market resources \(m_t\) and permanent income \(P_t\), the agent chooses normalized consumption: $\(c_t \in [0, m_t]\)$

    2.5 End-of-Period Assets¶

    After the consumption decision, normalized end-of-period assets are: $\(a_t \coloneqq m_t - c_t\)$

    2.6 Mortality Shock¶

    At the continuation perch, the agent faces a mortality shock \(\xi_t \in \{0, 1\}\) where: $\(\xi_t \sim \text{Bernoulli}(\pi)\)$ with \(\pi \in (0,1]\) denoting the survival probability. If \(\xi_t = 1\), the agent survives to period \(t+1\); if \(\xi_t = 0\), the agent dies and receives continuation value zero.

    2.7 State Transition¶

    For surviving agents (\(\xi_t = 1\)), the state at the arrival perch of period \(t+1\) is: $\(k_{t+1} \coloneqq a_t, \quad P_t \mapsto P_t\)$ where the twist mapping sends continuation perch state \((a_t, P_t)\) to arrival perch state \((k_{t+1}, P_{t+1}^{\text{prev}}) = (a_t, P_t)\).

    2.8 Initial Distribution¶

    At model initialization (\(t=0\)), the agent's state is drawn from: $\((k_0, P_{-1}) \sim \mu_0\)$ where \(\mu_0 = \text{pLvlInitDstn} \times \text{kNrmInitDstn}\) is the product of independent marginal distributions over initial permanent income and normalized capital.

    3. Bellman Equation¶

    3.1 Utility Function¶

    Let \(u(c) = \frac{c^{1-\rho}}{1-\rho}\) denote the period utility function with relative risk aversion \(\rho > 0\) (for \(\rho = 1\), \(u(c) = \log c\)).

    3.2 Three-Perch Decomposition¶

    Arrival Value Function¶

    At the arrival perch, the agent has state \((k_t, P_{t-1})\) and has not yet observed the deterministic income growth. The arrival value is: $\(V^{\text{arr}}_t(k_t, P_{t-1}) = V^{\text{dec}}_t(m_t, P_t)\)$ where the transition to the decision perch is deterministic: $\(P_t = P_{t-1} \cdot \Gamma, \quad m_t = \frac{R \cdot k_t}{\Gamma} + 1\)$

    Since there are no shocks between arrival and decision perches in this perfect foresight model, the arrival value equals the decision value.

    Decision Value Function¶

    At the decision perch, the agent observes \((m_t, P_t)\) and chooses consumption \(c_t\): $\(V^{\text{dec}}_t(m_t, P_t) = \max_{c_t \in [0, m_t]} \left\{ u(c_t \cdot P_t) + \beta \cdot V^{\text{cont}}_t(a_t, P_t) \right\}\)$ where \(a_t = m_t - c_t\). Note that utility is defined over consumption in levels, \(C_t = c_t \cdot P_t\), but the value function is expressed in normalized terms.

    Continuation Value Function¶

    At the continuation perch, the agent has state \((a_t, P_t)\) and faces the mortality shock: $\(V^{\text{cont}}_t(a_t, P_t) = \pi \cdot V^{\text{arr}}_{t+1}(a_t, P_t) + (1-\pi) \cdot 0\)$ where the agent survives with probability \(\pi\) and transitions to the next period's arrival perch with \(k_{t+1} = a_t\) and \(P_t\) serving as \(P_{t}^{\text{prev}}\) for the next period.

    3.3 Normalized Bellman Equation¶

    Define the normalized value function \(v_t(m_t) \coloneqq V^{\text{dec}}_t(m_t, P_t) / P_t^{1-\rho}\). Then the Bellman equation in normalized form is: $\(v_t(m_t) = \max_{c_t \in [0, m_t]} \left\{ u(c_t) + \beta \pi \left(\frac{P_{t+1}}{P_t}\right)^{1-\rho} v_{t+1}(m_{t+1}) \right\}\)$

    Since \(P_{t+1} = P_t \cdot \Gamma\), this simplifies to: $\(v_t(m_t) = \max_{c_t \in [0, m_t]} \left\{ \frac{c_t^{1-\rho}}{1-\rho} + \beta \pi \Gamma^{1-\rho} v_{t+1}\left(\frac{R(m_t - c_t)}{\Gamma} + 1\right) \right\}\)$

    3.4 Infinite Horizon Problem¶

    In the infinite horizon stationary setting, the value function satisfies: $\(v(m) = \max_{c \in [0, m]} \left\{ \frac{c^{1-\rho}}{1-\rho} + \beta \pi \Gamma^{1-\rho} v\left(\frac{R(m - c)}{\Gamma} + 1\right) \right\}\)$

    The effective discount factor is \(\tilde{\beta} \coloneqq \beta \pi \Gamma^{1-\rho}\), which incorporates both time preference, mortality risk, and the growth rate of permanent income.

    4. First-Order Conditions¶

    4.1 Euler Equation¶

    The first-order condition for optimal consumption at interior solutions is: $\(u'(c_t) = \beta \pi \left(\frac{P_{t+1}}{P_t}\right)^{-\rho} \frac{R}{\Gamma} u'(c_{t+1})\)$

    With CRRA utility \(u'(c) = c^{-\rho}\), this becomes: $\(c_t^{-\rho} = \beta \pi \Gamma^{-\rho} R c_{t+1}^{-\rho}\)$

    Rearranging: $\(\frac{c_{t+1}}{c_t} = \left(\beta \pi R \Gamma^{-\rho}\right)^{1/\rho} = (\beta \pi)^{1/\rho} \left(\frac{R}{\Gamma}\right)^{1/\rho}\)$

    4.2 Perfect Foresight Solution¶

    In the perfect foresight case, the consumption function is linear in market resources: $\(c_t = \kappa \cdot m_t\)$ where the marginal propensity to consume \(\kappa\) satisfies: $\(\kappa = \left(1 + \left(\frac{R}{\Gamma}\right)^{1/\rho} (\beta \pi)^{1/\rho - 1} \right)^{-1}\)$

    This can be derived by imposing the no-Ponzi condition and the transversality condition, along with the Euler equation.

    4.3 Envelope Condition¶

    The envelope theorem gives: $\(\frac{\partial v_t(m_t)}{\partial m_t} = u'(c_t(m_t))\)$

    This states that the marginal value of wealth equals the marginal utility of consumption at the optimal policy.

    4.4 Return-Impatience Condition¶

    For a well-defined solution, we require: $\(\tilde{\beta} R < \Gamma\)$ or equivalently: $\(\beta \pi R \Gamma^{-\rho} < 1\)$

    This return-impatience condition ensures that the agent does not want to accumulate assets indefinitely. When \(\tilde{\beta} R = \Gamma\), the consumption function has a non-zero lower bound (the agent never runs out of resources).

    4.5 Analytical Solution¶

    When \(\tilde{\beta} R < \Gamma\), the consumption function takes the form: $\(c(m) = \kappa m\)$ where: $\(\kappa = 1 - \left(\frac{\tilde{\beta} R}{\Gamma}\right)^{1/\rho} = 1 - (\beta \pi R \Gamma^{-\rho})^{1/\rho}\)$

    The value function is: $\(v(m) = \frac{(\kappa m)^{1-\rho}}{1-\rho} \cdot \left(1 - \tilde{\beta} \left(\frac{R}{\Gamma}(1-\kappa)\right)^{1-\rho}\right)^{-1}\)$

    5. Forward Operator¶

    5.1 Distribution Dynamics¶

    Let \(\Phi_t(k, P)\) denote the joint distribution of agents over normalized capital \(k\) and permanent income level \(P\) at the arrival perch of period \(t\).

    The forward operator \(\mathcal{T}\) maps \(\Phi_t\) to \(\Phi_{t+1}\) according to:

    Step 1: Deterministic transformation. Given \((k_t, P_{t-1}) \sim \Phi_t\), compute: - \(P_t = P_{t-1} \cdot \Gamma\) - \(m_t = \frac{R k_t}{\Gamma} + 1\) - \(c_t = c(m_t)\) (apply optimal policy) - \(a_t = m_t - c_t\)

    Step 2: Mortality selection. The agent survives with probability \(\pi\). For surviving agents: $\(k_{t+1} = a_t, \quad P_t^{\text{prev}} = P_t\)$

    Step 3: Distribution update. The distribution at \(t+1\) is: $\(\Phi_{t+1}(dk', dP') = \pi \int \mathbb{1}\{a_t(k,P) \in dk', P\Gamma \in dP'\} \, \Phi_t(dk, dP)\)$

    where \(a_t(k,P) = m_t(k,P) - c(m_t(k,P))\) with \(m_t(k,P) = \frac{Rk}{\Gamma} + 1\) and \(P' = P \cdot \Gamma\).

    5.2 Ergodic Distribution¶

    In the infinite horizon setting with constant parameters, if the system has a stationary distribution \(\Phi^*\), it satisfies: $\(\Phi^* = \mathcal{T}(\Phi^*)\)$

    However, because permanent income grows deterministically at rate \(\Gamma\), the level distribution of \(P\) does not have a stationary distribution—it shifts rightward each period. The normalized distribution over \((k, P/\bar{P}_t)\) where \(\bar{P}_t\) is the mean permanent income may converge to a stationary distribution under appropriate conditions.

    5.3 Cohort Interpretation¶

    Alternatively, we can interpret \(\Phi_t\) as the distribution of a single cohort over time. With mortality rate \(1-\pi\), the cohort size shrinks by factor \(\pi\) each period. The distribution of survivors evolves according to the forward operator above.

    5.4 Cross-Sectional Distribution¶

    If we consider a steady-state economy with overlapping generations where new agents are born each period with distribution \(\mu_0\), the cross-sectional distribution is: $\(\Phi^{\text{cross}}(dk, dP) = \sum_{s=0}^{\infty} \pi^s \mu_0^{(s)}(dk, dP)\)$ where \(\mu_0^{(s)}\) denotes the distribution of the cohort born \(s\) periods ago, evolved forward \(s\) periods under the policy function.
