    Methods and places¶

    Summary

    stage.places() answers the places of a stage, the operators at which a methods file may type a method, a grid or a handler, each with its kind. methods, asked of the stage, a graph, a builder or an operation, answers the components typed within it, each read by its place's path as the methods file writes it, stage.methods["policy"]. Typing a method changes no equation of the model and runs no computation; where the method brings lines, the operative builders hold them and the declared builders keep the stage file's. Every listing is printed by the package from applications/portfolio-choice/declaration/stages/cons/cons.bl and its methods file methods.bl, the addition beside it.

    Relationship with other documents

    Builds on — The stage: the object stage below; The binding verbs: methodize, which reads the methods file with the stage file. Used by — Parameters and settings: the settings the typed methods read. See also — Method types: every method word, the kinds of place it admits and the keyword arguments the package reads; Methodization: the methods file; API §2.4: every path of the stage object in one table.

    A method names a numerical procedure for one operator of a stage, such as the endogenous grid method for the policy builder, a grid the points at which the function at the operator is stored, and a handler the routine that carries the method out. The methods file types each at a place, an operator it can name, written by its path: a block, the Evaluation that stores the block's result, an operator inside a line, a line, or a definition. The method and the grid are components of the operator's signature and the handler a binding beside them; the signature of a builder prints the three beside the functions it reads and writes, and nothing in the package executes them.

    from bellman.stage import elaborate_stage, load_stage

stage = elaborate_stage(load_stage("applications/portfolio-choice/declaration/stages/cons/cons.bl"))
specified = (stage.calibrate("applications/portfolio-choice/declaration/calibration/portfolio.yml")
             .methodize("applications/portfolio-choice/declaration/stages/cons/methods.bl")
             .configure("applications/portfolio-choice/declaration/settings/cons.yml"))

    1 The places¶

    stage.places() answers one row per place, its path as a methods file writes it in the first column, address, and its kind in the second: a builder, by the id of its block; a line, by its address <block>.<line>, of the kind node where the line is marked !node and statement otherwise; an operator of one line, <block>.<line>.<operator>, of every line of a block, <block>.<operator>, or of every line of the stage, by the operator's head alone; and a definition, by its name. After the lines of the policy builder and of each backward value builder stand the places of the Evaluation that closes it, of the kind operator evaluate, where a representation of what the builder stores is typed: policy.evaluate and its one object, the policy, policy.evaluate[c], for the policy builder, and for a backward value builder the Evaluation as a whole, cntn_to_dcsn.evaluate, and each object it stores, cntn_to_dcsn.evaluate[V] for the value function and cntn_to_dcsn.evaluate[D_{m_d}V] for the derivative of it that the stage's derivative line states, so that the value and its derivative may be represented together or each on a grid of its own (Methodization §2). A representation is typed at these places and at no builder. The consumption stage writes no line marked !node. The kinds are read from the objects of the stage, whatever is typed at them; the derivative operator D is listed and admits no method. The listing reads stage after the verbs above have bound it, so the three lines of the methods file's block policy stand among the places beside the stage file's maximization.

    A path that names no place is refused when the methods file is read, as is an unknown method word and a known word at a place of a kind its row does not admit: a quadrature rule, for instance, belongs to an expectation and not to the policy builder. Method types states the kinds of place each word admits, and admitted_methods(stage, address) of bellman.stage answers the words admitted at one place. Typing a method runs nothing; where the method's row names a condition, such as egm-eligibility of the endogenous grid method, the stage records it among the builder's conditions, and the package does not check it (§3).

    2 The methods typed at a stage¶

    specified.methods answers every place at which a component is typed, one line each: the place, the method word with its keyword arguments, the grid where the place has one, and the handler, a quoted string, where one is named. The Evaluation of an !explicit builder at which no entry names a representation is listed with the closed form generated from the builder's entry, printed with that entry, generated from policy.method for the policy builder. Before methodization it is empty and prints none, as the stage elaborated from cons.bl alone shows; the binding verbs bind the stage they are called on, so stage and specified above name one stage.

    print(elaborate_stage(load_stage("applications/portfolio-choice/declaration/stages/cons/cons.bl")).methods)
print(specified.methods)

# grid of savings and the function egm of cons-port as its handler, and the
# three lines of the method, the inverted Euler equation with its Kuhn-Tucker

    The block policy of the methods file names the stage's policy block by its id. Its bracket types the policy builder's method, method : !egm, its grid of savings, grid : !Cartesian(a=(a_min, a_max, n_a)), and its handler, handler : "cons-port:egm", by paths relative to the block, and its three lines are the lines of the endogenous grid method, which the operative builder holds in place of the maximization (§3). The five entries after it stand at file level, each with the full path of its place: the representation of the decision value and of its stated derivative at the decision builder's Evaluation, one entry for both, with its grid of resources and its handler; the method of the forward builder from arrival to decision; and the handler of the forward builder from decision to continuation, which has no method word. The settings the two grids name are declared above the block with their sets (Parameters and settings §2).

    One place is read by its path as the methods file writes it: specified.methods["policy"] answers the components typed at the policy builder and specified.methods["cntn_to_dcsn.evaluate"] those at the decision builder's Evaluation. The answer is the place's record, which prints the word with its arguments, the grid as written and the handler, and holds each under word, grid and handler, with source, the data a representation reads, table, pairs, expression or own nodes (Methods §5), and generated_from, the entry a generated method comes from; a place at which no component is typed is refused with a KeyError that names the typed places.

    print(repr(specified.methods["policy"]))
print(repr(specified.methods["cntn_to_dcsn.evaluate"]))
print(specified.methods["policy"].handler, specified.methods["dcsn_to_cntn"].word == "")
try:
    specified.methods["cntn_to_dcsn"]
except KeyError as refusal:
    print(refusal)

    3 Methods at every level¶

    Every piece answers methods, the components typed at it or inside it, read from the typing entries of the stage; no piece keeps a copy. A builders graph, the backward graph stage.builder.bwd or the forward graph stage.builder.fwd, answers the components typed within it, and stage.builder those typed within both; a builder answers those typed at it or inside it, among them the representation at its Evaluation, which the builder holds under the key "evaluation"; and an operation or a line answers the components typed at it.

    print(specified.builder.bwd.methods)
print()
print(specified.dcsn.builder.bwd.methods)
print()
print(specified.dcsn.builder.bwd["evaluation"].methods)
print()
print(specified.builder.fwd.methods)

    cntn_to_dcsn.evaluate   !Linear   !Cartesian(m_d=(m_min, m_max, n_m))   "cons-port:_make_interp"
policy                  !egm   !Cartesian(a=(a_min, a_max, n_a))   "cons-port:egm"

arvl_to_dcsn   !deterministic

    A solution method that brings lines changes what the operative builder holds. The policy builder of the stage elaborated from cons.bl alone holds the maximization of the stage file as three operations and its Evaluation; after methodization, stage.builder.policy holds the three lines of the endogenous grid method as its operations, with the maximization kept under Declared on its card and in stage.declared.policy, the builder as the stage file states it (The builders graphs §1; Methodization §5). The card, the printed summary that denote() answers, prints the components typed within the builder under Method, before the source lines of both files and the definitions the builder refers to.

    print(stage.declared.policy.list())
print()
print(specified.builder.policy.list())
print()
print(specified.builder.policy.denote())
