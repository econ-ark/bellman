    A builder¶

    Summary

    A builder is the operator one block denotes, reached from the perch its edge leaves, the edge being the step from that perch to the next (The builders graphs §2): stage.dcsn.builder.bwd, a path that reads the pair of perches the builder joins, stage.builder["cntn", "dcsn"]. It answers the operations inside it, its inputs and outputs, its printed record, its drawing and its kernels, and opens to a finer grain by an attribute; an operation inside it is read by its name as a key. Every listing is printed by the package from applications/portfolio-choice/declaration/stages/cons/cons.bl, and §4 from applications/smooth-ambiguity-savings/declaration/stages/sa_savings/sa_savings.bl.

    Relationship with other documents

    Builds on — The builders graphs: the pairs and the perch paths that reach the builders, and the graphs that hold them. Used by — A piece: what an operation inside a builder answers. Formal account — Operators and builders: the operator a builder denotes. See also — API §2.4: every path of the stage object in one table.

    A builder is one box of a builders graph at the builder grain. Inside it stand the operations it applies in order, each a primitive operator such as a pullback, an aggregation or an evaluation; Operations lists the kinds. Where a line of the block is tagged !node, marking a named computation of its own, the builder also holds a sub-builder, a box that keeps that computation closed.

    from bellman.stage import elaborate_stage, load_stage

stage = elaborate_stage(load_stage("applications/portfolio-choice/declaration/stages/cons/cons.bl"))

    1 What a builder answers¶

    list() names the operations inside the builder in order of application, each under the key that reaches it, with its kind and the function it writes. signature() answers the functions the builder reads and writes, each with its type: the value functions alone, each on its own wire, while a derivative the file states for a value stands at the builder's evaluation and on no wire; for the decision builder it is the signature of the operator at a policy, and it names no policy.

    decision = stage.dcsn.builder.bwd
print(repr(decision))
print(decision.list())
print(decision.signature())

    Builder('cons', 'cntn_to_dcsn')
1  Pullback     primitive
2  Aggregation  primitive  → 𝑉
3  Evaluation   primitive  → 𝑉
in         𝑉≻ : Xa → [−∞, ∞)
out        𝑉 : Xm → [−∞, ∞)

    A builder at a policy is a backward builder or a push-forward stated at the policy function: wherever its lines are written in the control, the policy stands in place of that coordinate. There is one such operator for each policy, all with the same inputs and outputs, which is why the calculus writes the policy as a subscript on the decision operator, and why policy iteration, which holds the policy fixed while it computes the value under that policy, can be stated at all. Its signature() therefore says nothing about the policy, whose type is printed on the policy's own wire, the output of the policy builder (§2), and on no line of the builder at a policy. The name the builder uses for the policy is a free name, declared by no header of the builder: the elaboration resolves it to the line of the policy block that defines it, and the function that stands in its place in a computation is supplied later, by the method or by the realization, the later step in which a numerical routine is supplied for each operator, as calibration supplies the value of a parameter. The policy is not a parameter of the model, one of the declared constants that stage.par lists (Parameters and settings).

    denote() answers the builder's printed record, its card: the functions it reads and writes, the policy it is stated at, the law of motion it reads, the operations within it with their rules, the conditions the construction has not established, and the lines of the file it realizes. print(decision) prints the same card.

    print(decision.denote())

    The card's title names the stage, the builder's direction and the builder by the pair of perches it joins, cntn → dcsn, with the id of its block in brackets, while the policy builder's card is titled by its key, POLICY BUILDER policy (The builders graphs §1). The card's Operations section lists the operations within the builder, one entry each, with a blank line between two entries. An entry opens with its number and its name; the next line gives its inputs after in: and its outputs after out:, out: standing at column 40, or on the next line where the inputs or the outputs leave it no room there; a short line follows, and every short line of a card ends at the same column; then the rule the operation applies stands after rule:, with the set on which it holds, on …, at the right margin or under the rule; on an evaluation, a derivative the file states for the value stands after derivative:, with the line that states it and its condition marginal-reading. The lines state the wiring: the name after out: in one entry is the name after in: in the entry that reads it, and an output the file does not name is written, in the entry that reads it, as the number of the entry that produced it, (1), as a paper cites an equation by its number. Where the operations form a chain, each one's outputs being the next one's inputs, the same wires in the same order, the entries stand in the order they are applied; otherwise each stands after every entry that writes a wire it reads. On a builders graph's card each builder is an entry with no number, and its operations stand beneath it, numbered and set further in (The builders graphs §3).

    diagram() answers the drawing of the builder's interior, a Drawing; obligations() the conditions of its operations and of the lines it realizes; methods the methods bound at it or inside it, empty before methodization; and box the DisCoPy box itself, an object of the diagram library the package keeps its graphs in (The initial diagrams §2). In the drawing an aggregation is labeled Value aggregation 𝐺 where it produces value functions at a perch, as in the two backward builders, and Apply 𝐺 otherwise, as in the policy builder; where one builder holds several aggregations, as the policy builder does once the methods file has brought the lines of the endogenous grid method, the label names the statement each aggregates, Apply 𝐺 of the statement InvEuler, so that the two aggregations are told apart. The names of the operations carry the same distinction (§2). A pullback is labeled by the function it pulls back and the map it pulls back along: Pullback of 𝑉≻ along 𝑔 at 𝑐 in the decision builder, and Pullback of 𝑐 along 𝑚_d≻ for the identification of the methodized policy builder, which pulls the policy back along the map from each saving to its cash on hand and not along the law of motion (§2). A stage's two builders graphs, of which each builder is a box, are also read interactively in Google's Model Explorer, where each builder opens to its operations and a selected operation shows the rows of its card; the drawing remains the figure of this page (The view in Model Explorer).

    2 The policy builder¶

    stage.dcsn.builder.policy is the policy builder, the builder whose outputs are the controls. Its signature() carries the policy's type, the set of feasible policies where the file declares a feasibility correspondence, Γ here, beside the value functions it reads. The clause c(m_d) ∈ Γ(m_d) is the feasibility condition: the consumption the policy chooses lies in the feasible set that the correspondence Γ(x) := (0, x] of the file gives. On the symbolic stage the builder holds the maximization of the stage file's line optimality as three operations, the pullback of the continuation value along the law of motion, the aggregation of the objective and the maximization itself, and then its Evaluation:

    print(repr(stage.dcsn.builder.policy))
print(stage.dcsn.builder.policy.signature())
print(stage.dcsn.builder.policy.list())

    Builder('cons', 'policy')

    The methods file beside the stage file, methods.bl, types the endogenous grid method on this builder, with its grid of savings and its handler, and brings the three lines by which the method computes the policy (Methodization §1). After methodization the operative policy builder holds those lines as its operations, each named by the id of its line, while the maximization stays its declared line, which stage.declared.policy holds and the card prints under Declared (Methods and places §3); the signature gains the method, the grid and the handler. The listing reads a second elaboration of the file with its methods file, so that stage stays symbolic for the rest of the page:

    methodized = elaborate_stage(load_stage("applications/portfolio-choice/declaration/stages/cons/cons.bl",
                                        ["applications/portfolio-choice/declaration/stages/cons/methods.bl"]))
policy = methodized.dcsn.builder.policy
print(policy.signature())
for name in policy.list():
    print(name)

    The four operations state the policy as the lines of the methods file's block policy write it, and Operations §4 states how each such line is read. InvEuler:Aggregation is formed by the candidate line c[>] <= 𝑑𝑢𝑖𝑛𝑣(D_{a}V[>]) ⟂ a >= 0, which states the candidate consumption at each saving from the derivative term D_{a}V[>], the derivative of the continuation value in savings. Its clause ⟂ a >= 0, the complementary-slackness condition of the borrowing constraint, admits the interior value 𝑑𝑢𝑖𝑛𝑣(D_{a}𝑉≻(a)) alone at a positive saving and every positive value up to it at zero saving, so the operation's output is typed as a correspondence, a map to sets, Xa ⇉ ℝ₊₊ (Constraints §4.3). grid:Aggregation is formed by the grid line m_d[>] = a + c[>], the cash on hand at each saving and each value admitted there. identification:Pullback is formed by the identification c = c[>]: it reads the policy at that cash on hand and equates it with the candidate, a relation that must hold at every saving and every value admitted there, and its signature() answers the set of the feasible policies that satisfy it, which may be empty or hold several. Evaluation places that set in its declared carrier and selects no member; the handler of the method, cons-port:egm, stores its numerical representation of the function and asserts nothing about the set (Methods and places §2; Methodization §5).

    print(policy["InvEuler:Aggregation"].signature())
print(policy["identification:Pullback"].signature())

    A line marked !doc forms no operation, as the maximization objective of the portfolio stage applications/portfolio-choice/declaration/stages/port/port.bl is marked. An argmax line that is not marked !doc forms an operation of the kind Argmax, whose output is the set of maximizing policies, as optimality:Argmax does above and choice:Argmax does in the policy builder of applications/retirement-choice/declaration/stages/ret_choice/ret_choice.bl.

    3 A forward builder at a policy¶

    stage.dcsn.builder.fwd carries the measure at decision forward through the law of motion at the policy. It answers the same verbs, and state_choice_measure() besides: the joint measure of the decision state and the control under the policy the builder is stated at, the measure against which a simulated moment of a control is an integral.

    print(stage.dcsn.builder.fwd.list())
print(stage.dcsn.builder.fwd.state_choice_measure())

    1  Pushforward  primitive  → μ≻
mu
  ↦ measure on {m_d, c ∈ Xm × ℝ₊₊ | c ∈ Γ(m_d)} given by
    B ↦ mu({m_d ∈ Xm | (m_d ↦ (m_d, c(m_d)))(m_d) ∈ B})

    4 Grains and keys¶

    primitives answers the builder with every sub-builder opened and sub_builders the builder with each !node line kept as a box; each answers the same verbs. An operation is read by its name after the builder's own, the kind compared without regard to case, decision["pullback"], or by the whole name. A tuple of keys reads one level further down per element: the first names a sub-builder, the next an operation inside it. The consumption stage writes no !node line, so this section reads the smooth-ambiguity savings stage, whose decision builder computes a risk certainty equivalent risk_ce and an ambiguity certainty equivalent ambiguity_ce, each marked !node.

    sa = elaborate_stage(load_stage("applications/smooth-ambiguity-savings/declaration/stages/sa_savings/sa_savings.bl"))
print(sa.dcsn.builder.bwd.list())
print(repr(sa.dcsn.builder.bwd["risk_ce"]))
print(repr(sa.dcsn.builder.bwd["risk_ce", "expectation:1"]))
print(repr(sa.dcsn.builder.bwd.primitives))

    A sub-builder is one named intermediate result: the construction keeps the operations of a line marked !node inside a box of their own, named <block id>.<line id>, and the two sub-builders are the first two boxes inside the decision builder. In this stage the gross return Ψ is lognormal with an uncertain log mean ν, and γ is risk aversion. Each sub-builder has its own card:

    An output the file does not name is written as what produced it: an aggregation's result as the map it computes, (m_d, Ψ) ↦ 𝑉≻(𝑔≻(m_d, 𝑐(m_d), Ψ))^(1 − γ), and an expectation's as the expectation of the result it reads, named by the box that wrote it. The expectation box is where the shock is integrated: the backward graph takes the expectation over Ψ here, and the forward graph's corresponding box extends the measure by the same declared distribution (A piece §1).     5 The kernels of a builder¶

    kernels() answers one record of the builder's kernels, from which a numerical operator is built with no stage in hand: each operation in the order it is applied, with its printed kernel and the wires it reads and writes, and, where its kernel is an explicit function, its function with the names of its arguments and outputs, a derivative term the row reads standing among the arguments, G_policy_InvEuler(V≻, a, D_{a}(V≻); ρ) → c≻ for the candidate line InvEuler, or, for a row that states a relation its output satisfies with no explicit side, such as the identification of a policy block, whose compile_kernel() is refused under kernel-target (A piece §2), its printed relation; then the parameters with their values and the declared sets its operations are read on. The record refers to no stage, so cloudpickle saves it and another process reads it. The endogenous-grid step of the portfolio–consumption application is built from two such records of a specified version by one call of asva, the numerical toolkit installed beside the package, asva.egm(specified.dcsn.builder.policy.kernels(), specified.dcsn.builder.bwd.kernels(), savings, evaluation), the caller reading the savings grid and the evaluation grid from the settings. A piece §2 gives one operation's kernel as a function, and the lesson tutorials/topics/kernels.ipynb of the repository composes one step of the decision builder from its two kernels, compiles them with Numba and passes the values of a second calibration without compiling again; Solutions at the perches describes the dictionaries in which a solver keeps what the kernels compute.
