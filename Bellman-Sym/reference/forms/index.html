    Expression forms¶

    Summary

    An expression in a line of a stage file is built from names, numbers and arithmetic, and from nine named forms: the expectation E_{θ}(…), the maximization argmax_{c}(…), the implicit solution solve_{c}(…), the derivative D_{c}(𝑢) of a declared function and D_{a}V[>] of a value, the inverse Inv(𝑓), and the real functions exp, log, max and min. A shock's law is written on a line of its own, after ~. The table gives each form as a stage file writes it, what it computes, the object of the Bellman calculus it realizes where the calculus defines one, and the entry of the package that defines it. A block file, which a methods file names, holds two forms of block, the method block and the marginal block (§4).

    Relationship with other documents

    Builds on — Field equations: the lines in which these forms stand; Constraints: the inequalities and the ⟂ clause a line may carry. Formal account — Operators: the operations the forms denote; Equation parsing rules §§4–5: the variable a form binds and the reading of a law. See also — Shock distributions: the families a law names; Symbol conventions: declarations, domains and type words.

    1 Reading the table¶

    Each row is one form. written gives the first occurrence of the form that the package finds in the stage files under applications/, printed back as normalized .bl text by to_source of syntax/source.py, so that E_{Ψ,θ} in port.bl shows as E_{Ψ, θ}, with the file, or says that no stage file writes it; the arithmetic row gives the six signs of the grammar, and the laws row the call of each family as the module docstring of distributions/families.py spells it. meaning gives, for an operator written with a subscript, the words the package attaches to it in OPERATOR_MEANINGS of typing/line_unknowns.py, followed for D by its defining expression; for a named function of the definitions table, its defining expression; and for the arithmetic, the names the grammar gives its six operations, add, sub, neg, mul, div and pow. denotes links to the section of Appendix E.3 that defines the object the form realizes, and shows a dash where E.3 defines no such object, as for a real function applied at each point, such as exp. The laws row also shows a dash: each family has its own row, with its own link to E.3, on the laws page. read from names the module under packages/bellman/bellman/, its table and the key: BOUND_OPERATORS for the four heads written with a subscript in braces, and PRIMITIVE_DEFINITIONS["definitions"] for the named functions, each of which it defines exactly.

    A subscript in braces names the variable the form binds: the shock an expectation integrates, the control a maximization chooses, or the unknowns a solution solves for. The subscript of a derivative instead names an argument of a function the file has already defined, as in 𝑑𝑢 := D_{c}(𝑢), or, where the operator is applied to a value field written with its perch mark, a state coordinate of the perch at which the value is read, written without its mark: D_{a}V[>] is the derivative term, the derivative of the continuation value in the continuation coordinate a, and D_{a}D_{a}V[>] its second derivative. The term has this one spelling; D_{a}(V[>]) is not admitted. A subscript that names no state coordinate of that perch, or a discrete coordinate, is refused with the perch's coordinates named, and a derivative term of a value the block neither reads nor writes is refused as an undeclared name is (Refusals). A derivative term may stand on the right side of any line, where it reads a derivative, and on the left side of a line of a value builder's block, where it states the derivative of the value the builder writes, D_{m_d}V = 𝑑𝑢(c) (Field equations §2). The term applies an operator to a function the graph already holds; the package reads it and differentiates nothing.

    A law names a shock, or a tuple of shocks, on the left of ~ and a family applied to its arguments on the right. Shock distributions lists the families and the meaning of their arguments; Equation parsing rules §4 states how separate laws and a joint law are read.

    A braced form with any other head, such as max_{c}(…), is refused under the rule not-constructed of Refusals. A call of a name that neither the stage file nor the table defines, such as sqrt(x), has no definition and denotes nothing; define the function in the stage file instead, 𝑟(x : ℝ₊) := x^(1/2).

    2 Arithmetic¶

    Between the named forms stand numbers, the names of fields, parameters and functions, and the arithmetic of the grammar, syntax/grammars/stage.lark:

    Written Reading
    a + b, a - b, -a Sum, difference and negation. A leading minus negates the product that follows it: -a*b is -(a*b).
    a*b, a/b Product and quotient. A product is always written with *.
    a^b, a**b Power. The exponent is a single term, a name, a number, a call or an expression in parentheses: c^(1-ρ).
    ∞, inf Infinity; both spellings are one constant.
    (a, b) A tuple, a finite family of values.
    x[1], (V_young[>], W_old[>])[s] Selection from a family, by position counted from one or by the label a coordinate takes.
    𝑢(c) A function the stage file defines, applied to as many arguments as it declares.

    A power binds more tightly than a product or a quotient, and these more tightly than a sum or a difference; parentheses group. A line relates two expressions by = or by one of the inequalities <=, >=, <, >, ≤ and ≥, and may carry a complementary-slackness clause after ⟂.

    3 Forms that compile¶

    A kernel is the function an operation of a stage applies: the law of motion 𝑔 that a pullback or a push-forward applies, and the value aggregator 𝐺 that an aggregation applies. compile_kernel() answers the kernel of such an operation as a plain Python function, made from the source text that kernel().python holds. It answers one where the row writes its output as a function of its inputs, or states a relation with an explicit side whose function it compiles; a pullback or an aggregation whose row states a relation with no explicit side, such as the identification c = c[>], is refused under the rule kernel-target of Refusals, and a row that reads a derivative term, c[>] <= 𝑑𝑢𝑖𝑛𝑣(D_{a}V[>]) ⟂ a >= 0 in cons.bl, is refused under the rule kernel-form, naming the term, as a kernel that calls a function defined by D_{c}(𝑢) is (below). The function takes the kernel's arguments and then the parameters the kernel reads, and computes on numbers or on NumPy arrays. The values the calibration gives those parameters stand in kernel().parameter_values, None on a version not yet calibrated, and are passed at each call, so that a new calibration needs no new compilation. The text holds numbers and infinity, the kernel's arguments, the parameters, the arithmetic of §2, and the functions the stage file defines in closed form, such as 𝑢(c : ℝ₊₊) := c^(1-ρ)/(1-ρ), written as functions inside the one function. An expectation over a label shock, a shock whose law gives each label of a finite set its probability, compiles to the sum of the values weighted by those probabilities.

    The named functions exp, log, max, min and Inv, and a function the stage file defines by a derivative or an inverse, such as 𝑑𝑢 := D_{c}(𝑢), have no compile rule: their definitions state what they denote, and a kernel that calls one is not compiled, under the rule kernel-form of Refusals. The same function written in closed form, 𝑑𝑢(c : ℝ₊₊) := c^(-ρ), compiles. The other operations, among them an expectation that stands as an operation of its own, whatever the law of its shock, and a maximization, have no compiled kernel: compile_kernel() refuses them under the rule kernel-target.

    4 The blocks of a block file¶

    A block file is a file of blocks that a methods file names under block: (Methodization §4.1). It opens with a line naming its stage, @methods: cons, or with the stage's mark, @stage: cons, either of which the package accepts; it may define functions in closed form, 𝑑𝑢(c : ℝ₊₊) := c^(-ρ), whose kernels compile as a stage file's do (§3), and holds blocks of two forms, each written as a block of a stage file is (Stages):

    Written Reading
    [id=egm, (V[>] : ℝ @ !vlu) : !cntn -> (c : ℝ₊₊ @ !ctrl) : !dcsn] { … } A method block: its header carries the signature of a builder of the stage, here the policy builder, in terms of the values it reads and the controls at which its lines are read, and its lines compute that builder's output; a line may read the derivative of a value the header lists by the term D_{a}V[>], and the header lists nothing for it. A methods entry with block: and a method whose type takes a block, !egm here, binds it on the builder that on names; its lines become the builder's operations, and the stage file's line stays the builder's declared line.
    [id=dcsn_marginal, (c) : !cntn -> (V) : !dcsn] { … } A marginal block: its header is the value builder's header restricted to what its lines read, and its lines are derivative lines, each stating a derivative of the value the builder writes as the author asserts it, D_{m}V = 𝑑𝑢(c) here. A methods entry with !marginals and block: on a backward value builder records its lines at that builder's closing Evaluation as stated derivatives of the value, beside the representation bound there; the builder gains no wire and no box, and its signature is unchanged (Methodization §4.1).

    A block is matched to the builder its entry names by the perches it joins and the outputs it writes. Its header is checked against the builder's own: a method block's outputs are the builder's and its inputs are among the builder's inputs; a marginal block's inputs are among the builder's inputs and the controls at which its lines are read, and the values whose derivatives its lines state are among the builder's outputs. A header that does not match is refused under block-header-mismatch, and a function that neither the stage file nor the block file defines under block-name-undefined (Refusals). The demonstration's block file is applications/cons-port/declaration/methods/cons_egm.bl.
