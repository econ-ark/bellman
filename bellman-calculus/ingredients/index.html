    Ingredients¶

    Summary

    This reference card lists the principal symbols used in the calculus. It distinguishes fields from their representing functions, fixed-policy evaluation from optimization, and transition expectation from reward-bearing Bellman operators.

    Relationship with other documents

    Builds on — Stages and perches defines the stage notation. See also — Catalogues gives the longer type and method inventories. Used by — Symbol conventions and Bellman-SYM's Appendix C §1.5 record the source notation for these symbols.

    Stub

    This reference remains incomplete. The table summarizes the settled symbols; it does not define an additional source grammar or certify numerical-handler support.

    1. Reading guide¶

    Follow the linked chapter for the domain restrictions and assumptions. At a perch \(p\in\{\prec,\sim,\succ\}\), \(\mathsf X_p\) is a measurable state space, \(\mathsf V_p\) a chosen function space and \(\mathsf L\) the value domain. Let \(\mathsf A\) be the action space, \(\Gamma(x)\subseteq\mathsf A\) the feasible actions and \(\operatorname{Gr}(\Gamma)=\{(x,a):a\in\Gamma(x)\}\). The letters \(x,a\) denote a generic state and action; in the buffer-stock example \(m,c,a\) instead denote resources, consumption and assets after consumption.

    2. Symbols¶

    Symbol Meaning and type Defining relation or qualification Reference
    \(\mathcal F_\prec,\mathcal F_\sim,\mathcal F_\succ\) available information at arrival, decision and continuation \(\mathcal F_\prec\subseteq\mathcal F_\sim\subseteq\mathcal F_\succ\); distinct from state sufficiency Stages §1
    \(X_p,\mathsf X_p\) random state vector and its measurable state space \(X_p:\Omega\to\mathsf X_p\), where \(\Omega\) is the outcome space Fields and functions §1
    \(V_p,\mathrm v_p\) value field and representing function \(V_p=\mathrm v_p(X_p)\) almost surely when state-generated measurability and representative-domain conditions hold; \(\mathrm v_p\in\mathsf V_p\) Fields and functions §§1–2
    \(\mathrm g_\prec\) arrival transition \(\mathsf X_\prec\times\mathsf Z_\zeta\to\mathsf X_\sim\) Stages §1
    \(\mathrm g_\succ\) controlled continuation transition \(\operatorname{Gr}(\Gamma)\times\mathsf Z_\eta\to\mathsf X_\succ\) Stages §1
    \(g_1,g_2\) sub-transitions through an intermediate state \(g_\succ(x,a,\eta)=g_2(g_1(x,a),\eta)\) in the stated factorization Operators §10
    \(\Gamma\) feasible-action correspondence \(\Gamma:\mathsf X_\sim\rightrightarrows\mathsf A\) Stages §1
    \(\zeta,\eta\) pre-decision and post-decision shocks random elements in \(\mathsf Z_\zeta,\mathsf Z_\eta\) with declared conditional laws Stages §1
    \(\mathrm u,\beta\) utility and discount in the consumption example \(\mathrm u:\mathbb R_{++}\to\mathbb R\), \(\beta\in(0,1)\) Example
    \(\mathrm G_\succ,\mathrm G_\prec\) pointwise reward/continuation aggregators scalar continuation value is first: \(G_\succ(v,x,a,\eta)\) and \(G_\prec(v,x_\prec,\zeta)\); a statement may supply an inner map inside the shock integral and an outer map outside it (Stages §2.3a) Functions §1
    \(K_g\) pullback along a measurable map \(g\) \(K_gf=f\circ g\); functions on \(g\)'s codomain map to functions on its domain Operators §2
    \(\mathbb E_z\) shock integration maps an integrable function of retained arguments and \(z\) to a function of retained arguments, using their conditional law for \(z\) Operators §5
    \(B_\succ\) induced aggregator, \(\mathsf V_\succ\to\mathsf Q_\Gamma\) \((B_\succ f)(x,a)=\int G_\succ(f(g_\succ(x,a,\eta)),x,a,\eta)K_\eta(x,a,d\eta)\), where \(K_\eta\) is the conditional shock law; a map written outside the integral gives \(G^{\mathrm{out}}_\succ(\int\cdots)\) (Stages §2.3a) Operators §5
    \(Q_f\) choice-value function on \(\operatorname{Gr}(\Gamma)\) \(Q_f=B_\succ f\); \(\mathsf Q_\Gamma\) is the chosen class of such functions Operators §7
    \(\pi\) or \(\sigma\) feasible measurable policy, \(\mathsf X_\sim\to\mathsf A\) \(\pi(x)\in\Gamma(x)\); optimality is additional Functions §1
    \(\operatorname{argmax}\) specified measurable maximizing selection on its admissible domain maps suitable \(Q\) to \(\Pi_\Gamma\), the feasible measurable policies; requires attainment/selection Operators §7
    \(\operatorname{evaluate}\) evaluate a choice value under a policy \(\operatorname{evaluate}(Q,\pi)(x)=Q(x,\pi(x))\); separate from ordinary function-at-state evaluation Operators §7
    \(\operatorname{upper\_env}\) comparison of a specified candidate family selected outputs and completeness/optimality requirements depend on the declared problem and method Operators §7
    \(\mathfrak w=Wf\) post-decision expectation function, distinct from the operator \(W\) \(W:\mathsf V_\succ\to\mathsf V_a\), \((Wf)(a)=\int f(g_2(a,\eta))K_\eta(a,d\eta)\) where this state/law factorization is valid; discounting is outside \(W\) in this convention Operators §10
    \(\mathbb T_{\succ,\pi}\) fixed-policy continuation-to-decision evaluation \(\mathbb T_{\succ,\pi}f=\operatorname{evaluate}(B_\succ f,\pi)\) Operators §7
    \(\mathbb T_\succ\) optimizing continuation-to-decision operator \((\mathbb T_\succ f)(x)=\sup_{a\in\Gamma(x)}(B_\succ f)(x,a)\); the selection/evaluation factorization requires attainment Operators §7
    \(\mathbb T_\prec\) decision-to-arrival aggregation \(\mathbb T_\prec=B_\prec\) on specified function classes Operators §5
    \(\mathbb T_\pi,\mathbb T\) fixed-policy and optimizing stage operators \(\mathbb T_\pi=\mathbb T_\prec\circ\mathbb T_{\succ,\pi}\); \(\mathbb T=\mathbb T_\prec\circ\mathbb T_\succ\) Stages §2
    \(P_\prec,P_{\succ,\pi},P_\pi\) undiscounted transition expectations \(P_\pi=P_\prec\circ P_{\succ,\pi}\), distinct from reward-bearing Bellman evaluation Operators §3
    \(\mathbb M_\pi\) probability propagation under a fixed feasible policy \(\langle\mathbb M_\pi\mu,f\rangle=\langle\mu,P_\pi f\rangle\) for bounded measurable or absolutely integrable tests, with \(\langle\mu,f\rangle=\int f\,d\mu\) Operators §3
    \(\mathbb T_{\mathrm{period}}\) composition across a period's compatible boundaries maps end-boundary functions to start-boundary functions; a self-map only under the required identification Stage composition §2
    \(v^\star\) stationary infinite-horizon fixed point when established \(\mathbb T_{\mathrm{period}}v^\star=v^\star\); existence/uniqueness/optimality require hypotheses Periods §4
    \(Q_j(x)\) branch decision value at a common current state includes the branch's current reward and continuation calculation, distinct from a function on its continuation state space Branching §§4–5

    The integral rows require joint measurability and pointwise integrability on their full declared domains. Independent shock laws are special cases of the conditional laws displayed here. A scalar field domain does not establish those analytic properties. The post-decision factorization is the alternative timing stated in its source section, not a redefinition of the running example's continuation boundary.
