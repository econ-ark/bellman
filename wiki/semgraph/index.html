    semgraph¶

    semgraph means semantic graph: the constructed mathematical representation of the model. Use semgraph in current explanations. The parsed AST, or abstract syntax tree, records the constructor structure of the authored text; it is the input to construction, not the semgraph. Existing API names and historical quotations keep their spellings.

    This page is the terminology home for a semgraph's graphs, grains and wire bundles. Using semgraph as the Python module name introduces no second mathematical representation.

    The four graphs¶

    A stage holds four graphs, and each states which mathematical objects it represents. The boxes are operations, the ports are their individual input/output positions, and the wires connect compatible ports. A wire's type states what kind of object it carries. The stage reaches them as stage.initial.backward and stage.initial.forward, the two initial diagrams, and stage.builder.bwd and stage.builder.fwd, the two builders graphs. A builder is reached by the ordered pair of perches it joins, stage.builder["cntn", "dcsn"] for the decision builder, or from the perch its edge leaves, the edge being the step from that perch to the next, stage.dcsn.builder.bwd, a path defined as that one pair; the policy builder is read by its own key, policy (The builders graphs §2).

    Graph Objects and operations
    Backward initial diagram The authored blocks that compute values and the policy, on the fields they read and write.
    Forward initial diagram The authored transition blocks, likewise. The two initial diagrams are separate graphs and are never drawn as one.
    Backward builders graph Operators receiving continuation-value functions and producing objective, decision-value or arrival-value functions, as specified by their definitions.
    Forward builders graph Operators receiving finite measures over states and producing the measures induced by transitions, conditional distributions and a supplied policy.

    A field is a named quantity of a stage with a declared set, at its perch unless it is a shock, which belongs to no perch. Once shocks are declared, fields may be interpreted as random variables at the corresponding information sets. Representing fields as functions requires specified representing functions and sufficient state information. Sufficient means that the state contains the information needed for the relevant payoff arguments and conditional distributions. The required domains and integration conditions remain explicit; a field equation does not supply them automatically.

    Fields, values, functions and builders¶

    A field is a quantity defined over possible outcomes with an information context. Evaluating it at an outcome gives a value. Representing it by a function of a chosen state requires that state to retain the relevant information; measurability with respect to the full information set alone does not prove this. A backward builder acts on such functions, whereas a forward builder acts on measures describing state distributions; both are builders, the operators the blocks of a stage file denote.

    For consumption, the authored reward and continuation equations together with a = m_d - c give the choice value utility(c) + continuation(m_d-c) under the required state representation; discounting follows the authored equations. Consumption remains a free choice until a policy is supplied or selected. Evaluating the choice value at a feasible policy gives its one-step value update for the supplied continuation. The optimizing value is the supremum over feasible choices where defined; representing it by a policy additionally requires attainment and a measurable maximizing selection.

    These distinctions resolve a common ambiguity in compact Bellman notation: a field, its scalar value, its state-function representative and an operator on functions are different mathematical objects. A transition alone does not supply utility, discounting, expectations or a policy. Conditional distributions and integrability must be stated; a fixed independent shock probability distribution is a special assumption, not a consequence of diagram wiring.

    Representation and interpretation¶

    This entry owns the account of the four graphs of a stage, their grains and their value bundles. The two initial diagrams are the backward and the forward graphs on fields; the two builders graphs act on functions, backward, and on measures, forward. Whole builders, primitive operators and the sub-builders that authored nodes denote provide different inspection grains. Their boundaries need not nest, and arithmetic remains within the retained equation and function definitions.

    The origins of a box and the analytic conditions of the stage are answers of queries, not parts of the native graph. A missing interpretation rule is reported while the initial diagrams remain inspectable; display text is not parsed again to recover meaning.

    See The initial diagrams, Appendix C and methodization.

    The backward initial diagram preserves the authored policy block separately. A policy relation is not a selected function. In the decision builder at a policy, the policy reference is kernel data of the policy-indexed law of motion, value aggregator or conditional distribution. The forward decision builder is the push-forward through the policy-indexed transition \(g_{\succ,c}\), with any policy-dependent conditional distribution in its kernel data. Exact Evaluation reads no policy. The insertion of the policy is a factor of the pullback and of the push-forward at the policy, which their cards write out, 𝑉≻(𝑔≻(m_d, 𝑐(m_d))) and μ({m_d ∈ Xm | 𝑔≻(m_d, 𝑐(m_d)) ∈ B}), and no stage of the applications forms a separate PolicyInsertion row. The policy is typed once, on the policy builder's output, and no wire joins that output to the policy-indexed builders; how the backward builders and the policy builder are joined is stated at methodization. The forward builders graph uses transition and distribution definitions; it is not obtained by reversing every arrow of a reward-bearing Bellman operator. See backward builder and forward builder for the respective mathematical operations.

    Grains and defining operations¶

    A grain chooses which boxes are opened within a graph. It does not change that graph's mathematical objects. Elaboration constructs the two initial diagrams and the builder grain of the two builders graphs. Other grains are derived from those retained graphs when requested and kept on that stage version. A reader reaches the two finer public grains by an attribute of a graph, a builder or a sub-builder, primitives and sub_builders, as stage.builder.bwd.primitives; the combined and the factored grains are answered by semgraph.record, below.

    Principal grain What remains visible as a box
    Builder One complete builder operation, with its defining composition retained.
    Primitive Natural mathematical operations such as the pullback along the law of motion, whole scalar aggregation, conditional integration, probability transport, maximization and evaluation, where defined.
    Sub-builder (node in code) The sub-builders, the operators the statements marked !node denote, closed, with their definitions and actual dependencies. Unmarked equations remain wherever the composition needs them. A sub-builder's output is a function wire of its own, which a method can represent exactly or by an admitted approximation. The result of an operator occurrence inside an unmarked line (an E, an argmax, or a solve) is a wire the construction makes, not an authored one: it is present in the builders graphs below the builder grain, absent from the initial diagrams, and admits only the operation's own method. Attaching that method does not make the occurrence a node; !node alone creates the authored boundary.
    Factored The boxes that respect both the primitive and sub-builder divisions together with the local places at which a method may be typed. It opens a sub-builder around an operator to which a method may attach: an expectation, the maximization of an operative argmax line, or a row whose output is among its own unknowns.

    The complete transition g and scalar aggregator G are mathematical definitions on their declared arguments. Each has one definition record referring to its retained source equations, and every operation that uses the definition references it by identity: the pullback and the push-forward reference the forward builder's g, the value aggregation references G. DisCoPy is the diagram library used to store the boxes and wires of the graphs; the finest box of a graph is a primitive operator or a sub-builder. A box represents an occurrence and carries references to the definitions table: the transition, aggregator or distribution record, or the statement address. Its equation is printed from those references and its conditions are computed from that equation. Addition, subtraction and other arithmetic inside those equations are not boxes and do not become an additional grain. A !node inside g or G remains inspectable there.

    The grains need not nest. Two coordinate equations may together define one vector transition g; one named utility equation may define only part of G. Opening a sub-builder inside g cannot replace an outer operator on functions with a scalar box of another type.

    The additional combined grain starts at all declared builder outputs and traces their actual dependencies backward. On reaching a primitive operator or sub-builder, it keeps that box closed and continues along all its external input wires. It stops opening the box, not following its dependencies. Shared occurrences are visited once, and every output of a multi-output box is preserved. A sub-builder containing an expectation therefore remains closed until it is opened. An operation no declared output depends on is not drawn at this grain. This is a stopping rule, not a fifth graph or a claim that every decomposition has one finest grain.

    Value statements¶

    A backward block's statements are folded by structural recursion on their retained terms into primitive operators of three kinds on function wires, in the order the term nests them: the pullback of every continuation function along the block's law of motion, an expectation for every occurrence E_μ(·), and an aggregation for the arithmetic that combines terms carrying a value input. The arithmetic inside an occurrence's brackets is one aggregation before that expectation; the arithmetic outside is one aggregation per maximal pointwise segment. The fold never moves arithmetic across an expectation, so a statement that writes the expectation inside the line denotes the aggregation after the expectation, reading its result as a value input, and a statement that writes the aggregation inside the expectation denotes the canonical form with the aggregator inside the integral. Whether an aggregation's map is affine in its value inputs is never a condition of construction. A sub-term that carries no value input, a named function of fields, a parameter, an expression in fields and shocks, is part of the aggregation's map, never a box. The stored form of every equation is the retained term; the printed string and the compiled function are queries. A !node statement denotes a sub-builder, a cut of the builder's composition; a branch is a summand of the continuation coproduct, a bubble of the forward initial diagram and no wire.

    Operations inside a box¶

    A builder signature declares a builder's inputs and outputs. Opening any box must preserve its exact ordered input and output identities and types, and its operation on every admissible assignment of its free inputs. The replacement must also preserve the conditions under which the formulas are defined, local-variable scope, conditional distributions and shared dependencies. Those conditions are often called guards. A capture is an input used by a function definition besides its formal arguments.

    Each operation has one authoritative definition. Its expansion is derived from that definition; independently editable g/G definitions and expansions cannot supply competing meanings. A named numerical method is not by itself a proof that its output equals the defining operation. Native composition checks connections and types, not integrability, optimality or numerical convergence.

    The inspected DisCoPy 1.2.2 installation provides native identity, sequential composition (>>), tensor composition (@), and Diagram.substitute(i, inner) for replacing a selected diagram box. Hypergraph itself has no substitute method. Conversion with Hypergraph.from_diagram must preserve the ordered inputs and outputs and incidence, meaning which ports connect to which wires. The mathematical definitions remain in native Box.data; a drawing frame is not their replacement.

    Source order and evaluation order¶

    Source order is the order in which equations were authored. Queries may retain it for inspecting the source. The order of boxes in a stored Hypergraph is a separate representation detail; neither order necessarily puts a producer before its consumers.

    For an explicit acyclic computation, topological order puts each producer before every operation using its result. Independent operations may occupy the same layer, with source order breaking ties. If h supplies both y and z, evaluation requires h before y and z even when stored boxes list y, z, h. Backward traversal discovers this dependency; it does not mean evaluating consumers before producers.

    A cycle or implicit relation needs its own admitted interpretation. Drawing arrows differently, sorting boxes, or inserting a formal trace does not solve the economic problem or establish the existence of a fixed point. Equation addresses and method places remain unchanged when the presentation follows dependencies.

    Value bundles¶

    A value bundle is the tuple of value objects on one base: the maximal set of parallel wires of a backward graph that share their base coordinates and are written by one box and read by one box. Its components are the value objects the stage declares at that perch, and a bundle may hold one component only: the derivative of a value is no component, since it is read by the derivative term D_{a}V[>] and adds no wire. Parallel means that the wires coexist there, not that their values are independent, and grouping asserts no independence. The relation between a value and its derivative is written by the author on a derivative line, with the chain-rule factor where the law of motion between the perches is not the identity.

    The bundle is derived from the graph and stored nowhere; its type is the tensor of its wires, read as one function into the product of its components' result sets. One box acts on the whole bundle for one of two reasons. A pullback and an expectation apply one kernel to every component — the same law of motion, the same policy-indexed conditional distribution and the same shocks under the same conditioning — and an aggregation applies one vector-valued kernel, the pairing of the components' own maps, from the tuple of inputs to the tuple of outputs. A component that a later operator reads alone is a wire of the tensor carried on; no box unbundles it.

    A branch tuple is not a bundle. A branching stage has a continuation state space that is a disjoint union of the branches' state spaces, and functions on a disjoint union are a tuple of functions, one per label, so a branch also shows as several value wires side by side. The two differ in what is put side by side: a bundle is indexed by the value fields the perch declares and its components are functions on one state space, across which a kernel acts; a branch tuple is indexed by labels and its components are functions on different state spaces, across which the operator is a case analysis. A drawing shows a bundle as one thick wire labeled with its components and a branch tuple as one labeled wire per alternative meeting the aggregation of the selection line, and never the other way round. The thick wire says what the graph does, not what a header declares: at a graph's own boundary the graph stands for the box on its own side and one box is still required on the other, so the input V[>] of cons.bl's backward graph stands at the boundary, the decision builder and the policy builder each reading it, the policy builder by the derivative term D_{a}V[>]. In type notation the wire the arrival pullback of cons.bl writes is V∘g : m → ℝ; the drawing writes the same wire in the calculus spelling it uses for every other wire, V(g(m)).

    A graph — an initial diagram or a builders graph — fixes the mathematical objects, a grain selects which boxes are opened, and a bundle is read off the wires those boxes write and read.

    Native A @ B gives an ordered tensor of wire types. Grouping preserves each constituent's identity, type, domain, scope and dependencies. Tracing the grouped outputs together retains shared producers once. Reordering requires explicit swaps. Unbundling recovers the original ordered wires.

    Representing a bundle by one product wire is an additional structural operation. It requires defined packing and unpacking maps whose expansion recovers those same constituents. Tensor concatenation alone does not turn two wires into one, and boxes merely named pack and unpack do not cancel under native equality.

    A value bundle also preserves its components' separate domains. For example, bundling 1/x and x does not discard the latter's value at zero; forming one paired partial function on a common domain is a separate operation. Likewise, two separate coordinate distributions, or marginal measures, do not determine a joint distribution. Preserve the actual joint distribution and every coordinate needed by later conditional distributions. Copying one realized shock is different from drawing independently twice. Bundling may not conceal such dependencies or move a local variable outside its binding scope.

    Outputs computed within one builder¶

    Outputs of one authored builder can instead come from one joint local computation on that builder's effective domain: the inputs for which all its formulas are defined. With E denoting conditional expectation, V = E(W), U = V^2 and an unused local equation h = 1/U compute V once, then U, and restrict the builder to U ≠ 0. Both returned functions preserve that restriction, even though h is not an output. Their arguments follow the actual local dependencies and the checked declaration order; a control needed only by a domain condition remains an argument. This construction is distinct from placing independently defined functions on parallel wires: it does not impose one common domain on arbitrary bundles.

    Public query¶

    bellman.stage.semgraph.record is implemented for ordinary stages whose retained equations, domains, laws of motion and distributions have an admitted interpretation. It accepts an ElaboratedStage, the object elaborate_stage makes of a stage file. Import it with from bellman.stage import semgraph, the module that defines it. The operation returns the graph together with its inputs, outputs, conditions and parameter information.

    Its call signature is semgraph.record(stage, *, level, direction, grain="builder", assumptions=()). For example:

    from bellman.stage import semgraph

record = semgraph.record(stage, level="builders", direction="backward", grain="primitive")

    level selects "initial", the initial diagrams, or "builders", the builders graphs; direction selects "forward" or "backward" and is required. grain selects "builder", "sub-builder" (the code token "node"), "primitive", "combined" or "factored". An unsupported interpretation gives a diagnostic identifying the authored item and missing rule; the constructed initial diagrams remain available. The record states the graph and computes no solution.

    The immutable Semgraph record describes the native graph and its inputs and outputs. It adds no alternative graph language or second set of edges.

    Attribute What it retains
    .graph The native DisCoPy Hypergraph that the selected direction's handle holds, stage.builder.bwd.graph or stage.initial.forward.graph, at the grain the attribute primitives or sub_builders selects, with its records in Box.data.
    .inputs, .outputs The sorts of the graph's ordered input and output wires: identity, kind, perch, role, domain and coordinates.
    .obligations The conditions of the stage with their scope and status (required, assumed or established), the caller's assumptions in place of those they discharge.
    .parameters The sorts of the parameters that the terms of the graph's rows name, directly or through the functions they call; empty for an initial diagram.
    .calibration Supplied parameter values, or None before calibration.

    The record holds no copy of the origins or the methods: the origins of a box are its data, read with the box's kernel().origins, and the methods attached inside a graph are read with that graph's methods.

    An initial diagram includes any supplied functions still required through its retained definitions after its declared field inputs. Model parameters remain named inside those definitions, with their declarations and values available separately. Function arguments remain distinct from model parameters, captures and locally bound variables. The graph of a record is frozen, and a transformation constructs a new record. The ordered method inventory that a piece's methods reads retains the identities of its places across changes of graph and grain. Its entries retain their original stage meaning: an expectation's numerical integration method does not become a method for transporting probability measures. An empty inventory means no effective choices are attached; it does not by itself describe which setup steps ran.

    The optional assumptions value is a tuple of Condition records with status "assumed", matching requirements of the requested interpretation. An assumption cannot supply a missing policy or an unsupported structural rule. Conditions on formula domains remain explicit.

    Elaboration stores the two initial diagrams and the builder grain of each builders graph, or a refusal where one cannot be constructed. A query derives the requested grain from those retained objects and caches it for the stage version. ElaboratedStage has seven attributes: name, metadata, definitions, initial, _builders, bindings and provenance, the fifth the stored record of the two builders graphs, which stage.builder reads. The object is a stage occurrence, one stage file elaborated at one position of a period; a binding adds to it in place and returns it, and a different text is elaborated again as its own stage occurrence. Definitions retain source equations; queries derive their mathematical and display information.

    A stage is a term over a many-sorted signature and each graph is its image under the unique homomorphism (Goguen, Thatcher, Wagner and Wright 1977, "Initial Algebra Semantics and Continuous Algebras", §2 pp. 70–71 and §3.2 p. 76; in categorical form, Jacobs 1999, Categorical Logic and Type Theory, §2.2 p. 129, Theorem 2.2.5). A semantics given by a fold is compositional (Hutton 2023, "Programming Language Semantics: It's Easy as 1,2,3", §3 p. 4; Winskel 1993, The Formal Semantics of Programming Languages, §5.2 p. 60), and two homomorphisms that agree on a generating set are equal (Burris and Sankappanavar 2012, A Course in Universal Algebra, the Millennium Edition, Chapter II §6 p. 47, Theorem 6.2), so what the term determines is a query rather than a stored member. A grain is one presentation of the object, not a second object (Scott and Strachey 1971, Toward a Mathematical Semantics for Computer Languages, §0 pp. 1 and 4–5), and the fold cata f = f ∘ F(cata f) ∘ in⁻¹ opens a bubble (Leal de Freitas 2020, Recursion Schemes for Mathematicians, pp. 15–16). A box holds the parameters of its form and nothing else (Backus 1978, "Can Programming Be Liberated from the von Neumann Style?", §11.2.6 p. 622 and §13.1 p. 631). The definitions table is an environment that the bindings extend, with no store at elaboration (Schmidt 1986, Denotational Semantics: A Methodology for Language Development, §4.1 p. 54, §2.3.1 pp. 25–26, §5.1 pp. 66–68, §8.3 p. 170 and §10.2 p. 202); the methods form a second algebra held apart from the term, as effect handlers are (Plotkin and Pretnar 2009, "Handlers of Algebraic Effects", §4.1 p. 7 and §5.1 p. 8); and elaboration produces the internal representation once (Pfenning 2021, Lecture Notes on Elaboration, §1 p. L11.1). The definitions are the economist's objects, the feasible correspondence, the value space and the aggregator of a recursive decision process (Sargent and Stachurski 2026, Dynamic Programming, vol. 2, §7.1.1.1, pp. 206–207), and every record class the schema keeps is a class each tool that reads the stage must know (Wang, Appel, Korn and Serra 1997, "The Zephyr Abstract Syntax Description Language", §4 p. 11).

    Inspecting equations and complete maps¶

    stage.definitions holds the retained source statements as records of kind line, in authored order, including statements inside authored nodes, and stage.definitions.list() prints every record grouped by kind. The records of the lines retain the terms, conditions and addressed unknowns. Complete g and G records refer to these statements by identity; a printed preview does not replace them.

    stage.builder.bwd.primitives and stage.builder.fwd.primitives read the operations of the two builders graphs, one box per primitive operator, and stage.dcsn.builder.bwd.list() lists the operations of one builder in the order of application. stage.definitions.denote("cons:g:dcsn_to_cntn") answers the complete law of motion of a forward block, and the records of kind G the value aggregators of the backward blocks. A builder's inputs and outputs are stage.dcsn.builder.bwd.signature(), and the inputs and outputs of one graph are stage.builder.bwd.signature(), one record with two parts, inputs and outputs.

    denote() is the one verb below the stage, and view() is the stage's own: stage.view() prints the inventory of the occurrence, while stage.definitions.denote("<identity>") for a definition and stage.dcsn.builder.bwd["pullback"].denote() for a box each answer a record whose printed form is that piece's card. A card carries the typed inputs and outputs under Signature, the rule the piece applies, the operations of a builder in the order of application, the conditions and, on a methodized version, the method bound within the piece, and under a light line the lines of the stage file it comes from (semantics). print(piece) prints the same card. kernel() on a box prints the pointwise map the operator applies. Kernel arguments are pointwise values; the coordinates of a function on a wire describe where that function is evaluated. The two lists need not coincide.

    compile_kernel() on a box answers one plain Python function of a supported kernel, on a symbolic version as on a calibrated one, taking the kernel's arguments and then the values of the parameters it reads, which kernel().parameter_values holds in that order; kernel().python is its text. It is refused under the rule kernel-target for a box whose kernel is neither a law of motion nor a value aggregator, as an expectation's or an evaluation's is, and for a row whose rule has no side that is an explicit function of its inputs, as the identification of a policy block has none; it chooses no policy and solves no Bellman equation. Printing and these queries read the retained terms, without parsing source previews again.

    Names and parameters in diagrams¶

    Every wire of the backward builders graph carries a function or a set of functions. The set-valued case is the result of an Argmax row or of a row whose output is among its own unknowns; exact Evaluation preserves that set and selects no member. Function-valued wires include a value function such as V[>](·), its composition with the transition V[>](g(·)), an expectation's result, an objective function of state and control V(·, ·), the declared policy of a maximization π(·), the functions the policy lines define on the continuation states (c[>](·), m_d[>](·)) and a policy function c(·). States, controls and shocks are coordinates of the functions on backward wires, not separate wires. A kernel's arguments are the pointwise values used to define those functions, and the aggregation's results are the functions themselves. An evaluation operation (Evaluation) is the identity from a derived carrier into a declared carrier. Its object may be a function or a set of feasible policies, and it makes no selection. It reads no policy and inserts none. A numerical method may store an interpolant as its representation of a function without changing exact Evaluation. In a builder at a policy, the policy instead indexes the pullback, aggregation or conditional distribution in which it occurs.

    Pullback of the function along the law of motion g composes a supplied function with g, V ↦ V∘g; its forward partner is the push-forward through the law of motion g, μ ↦ g_#μ. Value aggregation G applies the complete value calculations defined by G and yields the value functions or, when a control is bound, the objective functions, using the pulled-back function or an expectation's result as its input (see aggregation). The policy builder uses the same kinds: its objective or residual is aggregated on the decision coordinates after the pullback along g. On the continuation grid, the inverse-Euler candidate line c[>] forms one Aggregation whose rule is the line's relation, an inequality or a complementarity included, and the coordinate line m_d[>] another, each reading the derivative of the continuation value at the saving by the term D_{a}V[>], with the author's derivative lines and inverse marginal utility and no automatic differentiation. Where the candidate line carries the clause ⟂ a >= 0, as in methods.bl, the methods file beside cons.bl, the candidate at each saving is a set: the interior value alone where the saving is positive, and every value up to it at zero saving. The candidate and the coordinate are then correspondences, printed 𝑐≻ : Xa ⇉ ℝ₊₊ and 𝑚_d≻ : Xa ⇉ Xm. The identification line c = c[>] forms the Pullback of the policy along the coordinate map m_d[>], equated to the candidate; its rule holds at every value admitted at each saving, the corner, consumption equal to cash on hand, among them (Constraints §4.3). The grid line is checked against the law of motion solved for the decision coordinate, and a line that disagrees is refused under the rule grid-line-consistency. An operative argmax line forms an Argmax row, whose output is the set of maximizing policies; and a solve line forms one Aggregation of its residuals. Expectation remains a separate operation. The kinds of the rows are Pullback, Aggregation, Expectation, Evaluation and Argmax (operations): a relation is the rule of a row whose output is among its own unknowns, and no kind of its own. A line attached to an operative line by a dotted id, as the first-order condition optimality.euler is attached to the argmax line optimality, is that row's property: it forms no row, no address and no method place, and it prints under the row.

    A value-type field sent backward, including a constant such as age or a bound, is a wire of the initial backward diagram. The corresponding builders graph carries its representing function. A constant field's empty state dependence does not make it a calibrated parameter.

    In a modular model, repeated declarations share their operators and calibration sources. Their supplied fields may differ, and methods and settings remain separate numerical choices. A stage occurrence's position is an address in the trellis, not an input to its equations.

    Parameters belong with definitions and calibration. They are held fixed when the functions act on states, controls or supplied functions, and are not drawn as separate semgraph wires. Their identities, domains and any supplied values remain inspectable. An uncalibrated parameter remains a symbolic quantity; its restrictions still apply. A parameter's absence from the displayed inputs does not remove it from the mathematical model.
