# API

> [!summary]
> The public names of `bellman.stage`, `bellman.period` and `bellman.trellis`, one table each, and every path and verb of the way in to the stage object: the functions that read, elaborate and bind stage files and [trellises](../../wiki/trellis.md), find the joins and the order of the stages of a [period](../../wiki/period.md) and draw periods, the table of method types, and the pieces of the stage object reached from the stage by name, each with what it answers, the type of its answer, one answer the package prints and where the package defines it. The attributes of the records these paths answer, such as a parameter's `value` or a kernel's `origins`, and the class `ElaboratedStage`, the module `semgraph` and the four refusal classes have no row. The tables are written from the package by the documentation's own script, so they list what the installed package answers. The pages below this index take the stage object one level at a time.

> [!info]- Relationship with other documents
> **Builds on** — [Stage tutorial](../01-stage.md): the stage file whose object these paths reach.
> **Used by** — [The factory API](factory/index.md) and [The stage](stage/01-the-stage.md): the pages of the two groups below.
> **See also** — [Operations](../reference/operations.md) and [Methods](../reference/methods.md): the kinds of operation and the method types a listing names.

> [!note]- Key terminology
> | Term | What it names |
> |---|---|
> | [Stage](../../wiki/stage.md) | One self-contained decision problem, written as one stage file, a `.bl` file; in Python, the object `elaborate_stage` returns for that file. |
> | [Perch](../../wiki/perch.md) | One of the three information sets of a stage: arrival `arvl`, decision `dcsn` and continuation `cntn`. |
> | [Field](../../wiki/field.md) | A named quantity at a perch, with a declared set: a state, a control, a shock or a value. |
> | Block | A group of equations of a stage file between declared inputs and outputs ([Stage tutorial](../01-stage.md#3-builder-blocks) §3). |
> | Initial diagram | The blocks of one direction of a stage on the fields they read and write, one graph backward and one forward. |
> | [Builder](../../wiki/builder.md) | The operator one block denotes: on value functions backward, on finite measures forward. |
> | Builders graph | The builders of one direction: `stage.builder.bwd`, the backward builders a solution applies, and `stage.builder.fwd`, the forward builders a simulation applies. |
> | Piece | A component of a stage reached by its name from the component that holds it: a builder, a block, a [sub-builder](../../wiki/sub_builder.md), an operation or a line. |
> | Operation | One primitive operator inside a builder, such as a pullback or an aggregation ([A piece](stage/05-piece.md) §1). |
> | Line | One equation of a block, read by its id; a line marked `!doc` states what the control is, is not computed and has no box ([The initial diagrams](stage/02-initial.md) §3). |
> | Grain | How far a graph's boxes are opened: one box per block, or one box per primitive operator. |
> | Wire | A typed connection of a graph, carrying one object: a field in an initial diagram, a function or a measure in a builders graph. |
> | Coordinate | A control a block lists among its inputs: its lines are written in it, and no wire carries it ([The initial diagrams](stage/02-initial.md) §3). |
> | Kernel | The pointwise object one operation applies: a law of motion 𝑔, a value aggregator 𝐺 or a declared distribution ([A piece](stage/05-piece.md) §2). |
> | Denotation | The mathematical object a piece denotes under the declarations of its file and the calibration of its version: a set, a map between sets, a measure, a relation or an operator; `denote()` states it, and the package computes none. |
> | Builder at a policy | A backward builder or a push-forward stated at the policy function: one operator for each policy, all with the same inputs and outputs ([A builder](stage/04-builder.md) §1). |

## 1 The way in

Every piece of a stage is reached from the piece that contains it, and each answers for itself, a verb meaning the same at every level that answers it. A builder is identified by the ordered pair of [perches](../../wiki/perch.md) it joins and read by that pair as a key of `stage.builder`: `stage.builder["cntn", "dcsn"]` is the backward builder from continuation to decision, and the policy builder is read by its own key, `stage.builder["policy"]`. Each perch reaches the builders of the edge that leaves it, `stage.dcsn.builder.bwd`, a path defined as that one pair, and the pages below write a builder by its perch path ([The builders graphs](stage/03-build.md) §§1 and 2). Every other piece is read by its name as a key, `stage.dcsn.builder.bwd["pullback"]`; one parameter, one setting and one bound method by the name their file writes, `stage.par["ρ"]`, `stage.settings["n_a"]` and `stage.methods["policy"]`; and the value alone of a parameter or a setting by the same name as an attribute, `stage.par.ρ` and `stage.settings.n_a`. A graph, a builder and a sub-builder open to a finer grain by an attribute, `primitives` or `sub_builders`. The verbs are `view()` for the stage's printed view, `list()` for the pieces inside, `denote()` for the piece's printed record, `signature()` for its inputs and outputs, `diagram()` for its drawing, `kernel()` and `compile_kernel()` for the rule an operation applies, `kernels()` for the kernels of every operation of a builder or a sub-builder, as one record that refers to no stage, and `methods` for the methods bound within it. The stage answers no `signature()` or `diagram()`; `stage.initial` answers `list()` and `methods` alone, and `stage.builder` these two and `view()`; a perch answers no verb, and the builders of a perch, `stage.dcsn.builder`, answer `list()` alone; only the stage, `stage.builder`, `stage.par` and `stage.settings` answer `view()`; an operation answers no `diagram()`; a block answers no `kernels()`, which the builder of the same name answers; and a line marked `!doc` answers `denote()` and `methods` alone.

Beside the drawing that `diagram()` answers, a stage or a trellis is read interactively in Google's Model Explorer through the functions `explore` and `write_model_explorer`, which stand in `bellman.stage` and in `bellman.trellis` and are no verbs of a piece: [The view in Model Explorer](stage/10-model-explorer.md).

The registry of the representation families stands in the submodule `bellman.stage.representations`, whose names the tables of §2 do not hold: the class `RepresentationFamily` describes one family, `register(family)` adds it, `unregister(word)` removes it, and `families()` answers the registered families by word; the method table `bellman.stage.method_types` lists a registered family as it lists the package's own rows ([Methodization](../04-methodization.md) §3.4).

## 2 Every path, in four tables

One row per public function and per public constant of `bellman.stage`, `bellman.period` and `bellman.trellis`, in one table per namespace, then one row per path and verb of the way in, each written from what the package answers; a class or a module that a namespace exports, such as the class `ElaboratedStage`, has no row. Every table has five columns:

- **path** is what you type: a function with the parameters a call must give; the stage itself, `stage`, the object `elaborate_stage` returns; or an attribute or verb of an object reached from `stage`, at the path that reaches the object. A reading by a key is written with the name the package gives the key, `stage.par[name]`, and a reading by an attribute, by which a parameter or a setting answers its value under its own name, with that name in angle brackets, `stage.par.<name>`.
- **what it answers** is the first sentence of the docstring the package gives the function, the attribute or the verb. An attribute or a reading by a key with no docstring of its own takes the first sentence of the docstring of the class of its answer, where that class is the package's own and the answer is no frozen copy, the immutable copy a stage holds of an object of DisCoPy, the library the package builds its graphs with; otherwise it takes the sentence of the docstring of the class holding it that names the attribute or writes a key. The row of the stage itself takes the first sentence of the docstring of its class. A constant, such as the method table or a perch word, takes the first sentence of the docstring of its class where the package defines that class, and otherwise that of the comment above the line that assigns it.
- **type** is the class of the answer: for a frozen copy, the class of the object it copies, `BellmanHypergraph`, `Builder` or `Box`; otherwise the answer's class, or, where that class's name begins with an underscore, the first class it inherits from whose name does not, such as `Mapping` for the method table.
- **example** is the call, where the path takes an argument or a key, and the answer as the package prints it, on one line: the first line it prints, each run of white space written as one space and a function's memory address left out, cut to its first 75 characters where it runs past 76. The mark … stands for what the cell leaves out, the rest of a cut line or the later lines of an answer that prints several. Where the package refuses the path, the type is a dash and the example names the object that refuses it, with the refusal's class and message.
- **defined in** is the module, by its path in the package, and the name of the function, the class, the attribute or the verb that the row describes.

The row of the stage itself and every row of an object reached from it, the rows of §2.4, are printed on the specified version of the stage file `applications/portfolio-choice/declaration/stages/cons/cons.bl`, calibrated by `applications/portfolio-choice/declaration/calibration/portfolio.yml`, methodized by `applications/portfolio-choice/declaration/stages/cons/methods.bl`, the methods file beside the stage file, and configured by `applications/portfolio-choice/declaration/settings/cons.yml`; the example of `calibrate` is applied to the symbolic stage, that of `methodize` to the calibrated version and that of `configure` to the methodized one. An object has the attributes and verbs of its class, so the rows of one object name what every object of its kind answers, and each kind has its rows at the paths named here: those of a graph stand at `stage.initial.backward`, `stage.builder.bwd` and `stage.builder.fwd`; those of a builder at `stage.builder["cntn", "dcsn"]`, except `state_choice_measure()`, the forward decision builder's joint measure of state and choice, at `stage.builder["dcsn", "cntn"]`; those of an operation at `stage.builder["cntn", "dcsn"]["pullback"]`; those of a line with a box at `stage.initial.backward["cntn_to_dcsn"]["value"]`, the line read from its block by its id; and those of a line marked `!doc`, which the consumption stage does not write, at `stage.initial.backward["policy", "objective"]` of the portfolio stage `applications/portfolio-choice/declaration/stages/port/port.bl`, whose maximization is so marked. The mapping `stage.builder` has one row for each pair it reads on `cons.bl`, and the builders of a perch have their rows at each of the three perches, `stage.arvl.builder`, `stage.dcsn.builder` and `stage.cntn.builder`, since each perch answers or refuses its paths for reasons of its own. A block shares its class with a builder, so only its reading by a key has a row, at `stage.initial.backward["cntn_to_dcsn"]`. The functions of `bellman.stage` are printed on `cons.bl`: in their calls, `text` is the text of the file, `record` the record `load_stage` reads from it, and `stage` the symbolic stage `elaborate_stage(record)`, not the specified version of §2.4. The functions of `bellman.period` and `bellman.trellis` are printed on the trellis file `applications/cons-port/declaration/trellis.cons-port.yml`, where in a call `syntactic` is the trellis the file writes, `elaborated` its elaborated trellis and `periods` that trellis's periods, or on the durables [registry](../../wiki/spec_factory.md) `applications/durables/declaration` with the [recipe](../../wiki/spec_factory.md) `spec.two-stages.yml`. The functions of a period are printed on `periods[0]`, and `inter_period_joins` on the file's inter-period mapping, `mapping`, with `periods[1]`, the period appended later and so earlier in time, and `periods[0]`. The functions of the view in Model Explorer are printed in `bellman.stage` on the symbolic stage of `cons.bl` and in `bellman.trellis` on `elaborated`; `explore` and `write_model_explorer` each write their two files into a folder `model-explorer` inside a temporary folder, which is removed afterwards, and answer their paths relative to the temporary folder. A trellis is a plain Python dictionary, whose printed form is Python's own.
