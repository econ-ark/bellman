# Methods

> [!summary]
> A numerical method is written into the signature of the operator it serves. Beside its field component, the functions or measures an operator reads and writes, the signature carries two further components, the operator's method and its grid, each written as a typing of the operator's name. Operators compose over their field components alone; the labels of a composite are kept as a list and never combined. The method is typed by the kind of operator, the grid is the finite set of points at which a function is stored, and the handler, the program that computes the method, stands outside the calculus. Two erasures recover the model: one forgets the labels, the other forgets the methods file. Nothing is differentiated by the language.

## 1 A method and its place

A stage file states a model: its fields, their laws of motion, its values and its maximization ([Stages and perches](./03-Stages%20and%20perches.md)). Computing the model requires choices the model does not make: how an expectation over a shock is integrated, how a function is stored between the points at which it is computed, how a maximization is carried out. A **method** is one such choice, named by a word. The word `!gauss_hermite` integrates an expectation by Gauss–Hermite quadrature, `!Linear` stores a function by piecewise linear interpolation, and `!egm` computes the policy by the endogenous grid method. A method serves one **operator** of the stage, an operation such as an expectation or an evaluation, or a builder composed of such operations ([Operators and builders](./05-Operators%20and%20builders.md) §6), and it is written on that operator's name and on nothing else: not on a field, which is a quantity and not a computation, and not on a side of a block header, the inputs before the arrow or the outputs after it, whose perch word types every coordinate listed on it.

The economist writes the method in a methods file, an [addition](../wiki/addition.md) to the stage file, as a typing of the operator's method component, `policy.method : !egm`, and the operator's grid beside it, `policy.grid : !Cartesian(a=(a_min, a_max, n_a))`; the Bellman-SYM page on methodization gives the written forms. This page gives the mathematics of the two entries: the signature of an operator with its components (§2), how such operators compose (§3), what kind of object a method is (§4) and a grid is (§5), where the settings and the handler stand (§§6, 7), how the model is recovered from the methodized stage (§8), what a solution method changes in a stage (§9), and what no method may do (§10).

A method is not an argument of its operator. Two methods at one operator read the same inputs and write the same outputs, and they differ in the numbers they produce. Piecewise linear interpolation of $x^2$ on the grid $\{0, \tfrac12, 1\}$ gives $\tfrac18$ at $x = \tfrac14$, where the function is $\tfrac1{16}$; on the grid $\{0, \tfrac14, \tfrac12, \tfrac34, 1\}$ it gives $\tfrac1{16}$, since $\tfrac14$ is then a node, and a cubic interpolant on the first grid gives a third number. The stored function changes with the method and the grid, while its signature $\mathbb R \to \mathbb R$ does not.

## 2 The signature with its components

Every operator of a stage has a **field component**, written $X \to Y$: $X$ is the sort of what it reads and $Y$ the sort of what it writes, each a function space or a space of measures on the states of a perch ([The stage term](./APPX-C-STAGE-TERM.md) §1). The expectation of the buffer-stock arrival builder reads a function of the decision state and the shocks and writes a function of the arrival state; the policy builder reads the continuation value and writes the policy. The field component decides what composes with what, and the inputs and outputs are the only part of the operator the model fixes.

The signature of a methodized operator $\omega$ carries two further components,

$$
\omega : M,\ G,\ X \to Y,
$$

the **method component** $M$ and the **grid component** $G$. Each is a **classifier** in the sense of [Kinds and sorts](./APPX-D-KINDS-AND-SORTS.md) §2, D6: a function from the operators of the stage to a set of labels, whose fibers are the operators that share a label. $M$ names the numerical method, chosen among the methods admitted at an operator of $\omega$'s operation kind (§4), and $G$ names the grid on the domain of the function the operator stores or evaluates (§5). Each is assigned by one typing entry of the operator's name, `policy.method : !egm` and `policy.grid : !Cartesian(a=(a_min, a_max, n_a))`. An operator that no entry names carries the **identity label** in that component. The handler, the program that computes the method, is no component of the signature; it is a binding beside the term (§7).

Formally, let $\Sigma$ be the free category on the stage's operators: the graph whose vertices are the sorts, the types of the wires with their perches, products of sorts among them, and whose edges are the operation symbols, each from its input sort to its output sort ([The stage term](./APPX-C-STAGE-TERM.md) §1). A morphism of $\Sigma$ is a finite path of operation symbols, composed by concatenation, and a functor out of $\Sigma$ is fixed by its values on the edges, since a path is a finite sequence of them. A **label** is a pair of a method word with its arguments and a grid. Let $\mathcal M$ be the free monoid on the labels, the finite lists of labels with concatenation as the tensor, written $\odot$, and the list with no entries as the unit, the identity label; read $\mathcal M$ as a strict monoidal category whose only morphisms are identities, acting trivially on $\Sigma$, $M \bullet X = X$. The parametrized morphisms of Capucci, Gavranović, Hedges and Rischel (2022, Definition 2, p. 236) form the bicategory $\mathrm{Para}_{\mathcal M}(\Sigma)$:[^para] its objects are those of $\Sigma$, and a 1-cell from $X$ to $Y$ with parameter $M$ is an object $M$ of $\mathcal M$ together with a morphism $M \bullet X \to Y$, which under the trivial action is a path $X \to Y$ carrying the list $M$. Its 2-cells are identities, $\mathcal M$ having no others, so it is a category. The **methodized signature** is $\Sigma_m = \mathrm{Para}_{\mathcal M}(\Sigma)$, and a **labeled generator** is an operation symbol $\omega$ with the one-element list $[\ell]$, $\ell$ a label admitted at $\omega$ (§4), or with the identity label. A methodized operator is therefore its field component together with its label. The label is a factor of its own beside $X$ and no classifier of $X$: a method is written on the operator, never on the field it reads, and never on the output field, since a wire carries no label.

## 3 Composition over the field component

Two operators compose when the output sort of the first is the input sort of the second, $\operatorname{cod}(\omega_1) = \operatorname{dom}(\omega_2)$, and the condition reads the field components alone. The field component of the composite is the composite of the two maps. Its method component is the list of the two labels, kept and not composed, and its grid component the list of the two grids. A builder composed of a pullback, an aggregation and an evaluation carries the three labels of those operations; the stage operator carries the labels of its builders. Nothing of the model is lost in a composite, because the erasure of §8 returns the composite of the field components.

The labels are not composed because two labels have no product that is again a method: the catalogue admits a method at a generator of one of the operation kinds of §4, and a composite formed in $\Sigma_m$ is of none of those kinds. A builder that admits a solution method, such as the policy builder under `!egm`, is one generator at the operative level, whose lines the method supplies (§9).

Formally, the composite of $X \to Y$ with parameter $M_1$ and $Y \to Z$ with parameter $M_2$ is the morphism $X \to Z$ with parameter $M_2 \odot M_1$, the two base morphisms composed and the two parameters tensored (Capucci, Gavranović, Hedges and Rischel 2022, Definition 2, item 3, p. 236).[^para] With concatenation as the tensor, the parameter of a path of labeled generators is the list of their labels, the later generator's first, and the identity label of a generator without an entry adds nothing to it. The list is the ruling's collection: each label stands in it beside the others, and none is composed with another.

## 4 The method as a type over the kind of operator

Which methods an operator admits depends on what kind of operator it is. A quadrature rule is admitted at an expectation, an interpolant at the Evaluation that stores a builder's result, a solution method such as the endogenous grid method at the policy builder, a root finder at a maximization written as a first-order condition, and a discretization of a law at the expectation that integrates against it. The derivative operator `D` admits no method, because the language differentiates nothing (§10).

Formally, $\mathrm{Method}$ is a family of types indexed by the operation kinds: $\mathrm{Method}(\kappa)$ is the type of the methods admitted at an operator of kind $\kappa$, and the method component of an operator $\omega$ of kind $\kappa$ is a term of $\mathrm{Method}(\kappa)$ or the identity label. The type of the label depends on the operator it labels, which is what a **dependent type** means here. The family is indexed by the operators through their kinds: the labels a method assignment may place on a generator $\omega$ of kind $\kappa$ are those of $\mathrm{Method}(\kappa)$ and the identity label, the **fiber** of the family over $\omega$, in the sense in which a family of sets indexed by a set is displayed as a map onto the index set, each set standing over its index (Jacobs 1999, §1.1, pp. 20–21).[^jacobs] A method is a term of the fiber, built by its word with keyword arguments: `!gauss_hermite(n_ψ=n_ψ_nodes, n_ξ=n_ξ_nodes)` is a term of $\mathrm{Method}(\mathrm{Expectation})$ at an expectation over two shocks, `!Linear(extrap=clamp)` a term of $\mathrm{Method}(\mathrm{Evaluation})$, and `!egm` a term of the type at the policy builder. The catalogue of method words with the kinds each admits is the Bellman-SYM reference page on method types. Methods have no subtyping: no method stands for another.

Every argument of a method word is a **keyword argument**, written `key=value`, whose value is the name of a declared setting (§6), a number, a quoted string, or a word with its own arguments; a numerical choice that is to be configured is written as a declared setting's name. The catalogue row lists the keywords the package reads; a further keyword is read by the handler that computes the method (§7), so that an argument a handler needs can be written without a change to the language. What a method does outside its grid, such as extrapolation or clamping at the bounds, is a keyword argument of the method word and no part of the grid.

A method does one of three things to the stage, according to the operator it stands on. A **representation**, such as `!Linear` or `!pchip`, stores a function over the grid its grid component names: at the Evaluation that closes a builder, one entry for everything the Evaluation stores or one entry per stored object, `evaluate[V[<]].method` and `evaluate[D_{k}V[<]].method`, each with its own grid; or at a line marked `!node`, a computation inside a block that the author names so that a method can stand on it; or at a declared function. A **solution method**, such as `!egm`, stands on a builder and supplies the lines by which the builder is computed (§9); `!vfi` is a solution method that supplies no lines. A method of an **operator of a line** fixes how that operation is computed: a quadrature or a discretization of a law at an expectation, `!gauss_hermite` or `!tauchen`, and a root finder or a search at a maximization.

## 5 The grid as the discretized domain

A **grid** is the finite set of points of a function's domain at which a method computes and stores the function; between the points the representation supplies the values. The grid is written as its own component of the operator's signature, the discretized domain, by the entry `policy.grid : !Cartesian(a=(a_min, a_max, n_a))`: the kind `!Cartesian` is a product of one node set per coordinate, keyed by the coordinate's name, and a factor is a triple of declared settings, the lower bound, the upper bound and the count, with an optional spacing word, or a declared node set, or the expectation operator whose quadrature nodes the coordinate takes, or, for a control, its feasible set with a count. The wiki entry [grid](../wiki/grid.md) lists the four shapes of a factor with their spellings. No number stands in the entry: the bounds and counts are settings declared in the methods file (§6).

When an operator writes no grid entry, its grid is `!Cartesian` over every state coordinate of the domain of the function it holds, in declared order, and no number is supplied by default. For an Evaluation these are the states of the perch at which the stored function stands. For a solution method they are the states its rows name: for the endogenous grid method, the states of the continuation perch, the input perch of the policy builder, since the method's grid is the predetermined grid of end-of-period assets from which the points of the policy are generated (Carroll 2006, §2.2, p. 315).[^carroll] For the same reason the representation of the policy under `!egm` writes no grid: its knots are the pairs the method computes.

A grid is a numerical object and no part of the model. A discrete set that belongs to the economics, such as the set of housing sizes a household chooses among, is a domain declared in the stage file, and the scalars that define it are parameters ([Parameters and settings](./catalogues/parameters-settings.md)); a grid approximates a continuous domain and names settings. At configuration the package checks each bound pair ordered and inside the coordinate's declared set, each count at least two and each node set strictly increasing. It checks nothing about accuracy or about the region of the domain the model's distribution visits; those are propositions about the method, which the package does not check.

Formally, a grid on a domain $X$ is a finite subset $G \subset X$ with its inclusion $\iota : G \to X$. Restriction $r(f) = f \circ \iota$ sends a function on $X$ to its values at the nodes, $r : \mathbb R^X \to \mathbb R^G$. A representation is an interpolant $s : \mathbb R^G \to \mathbb R^X$ that reproduces the values at the nodes,

$$
r \circ s = \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb R^G},
$$

so that $s$ is a **section** and $r$ a **retraction** in the sense of Adámek, Herrlich and Strecker (2004, Definitions 7.19 and 7.24, pp. 106–107),[^ahs] and $\mathbb R^G$, the values at the nodes, is a retract of the function space. The composite $e = s \circ r$ on $\mathbb R^X$ is idempotent, $e \circ e = s \circ (r \circ s) \circ r = e$, and its fixed points are exactly the functions in the image of $s$, the represented functions. Piecewise linear interpolation and the shape-preserving cubic are two sections over one grid: they share $G$, $r$ and the retract $\mathbb R^G$, and they differ in the section and its image. The grid is the object the retract is built on, and the method is the section, which is why the grid and the method are two components. The inclusion $\iota$ is a map of sets and establishes no subsort relation between the grid and the domain; [Kinds and sorts](./APPX-D-KINDS-AND-SORTS.md) §3 lists the grid as a sort of its own.

## 6 Settings

A **setting** is a value a method reads: the bounds and the count of a grid, a tolerance, the number of quadrature nodes, a rule chosen among finitely many labels. The methods file declares each setting it reads at file level, in the form a stage file declares a parameter, with its set and a description, `n_a : ℕ`, `a_min : Xa` for a bound on the set `Xa` the stage declares for the savings coordinate, `tol_v : ℝ₊₊`. The values stand in the settings file, and `configure` binds them as `calibrate` binds a parameter's value, refusing a value outside the declared set and a name with no value. A setting is told from a parameter by the file that declares it: a parameter is declared by the stage file and defines the model; a setting is declared by the methods file and configures a method. No value is part of a signature: the grid component and the method word name the settings, and the configured values stand in the bindings. The wiki entry [settings](../wiki/settings.md) gives the declarations, the scope of a name and the table `stage.settings`.

## 7 The handler and the realization

A **handler** is the program that computes a method, named by its package and its function and written as a binding beside the term, `policy.handler : "cons-port:egm"`, at any operator that admits a method, with or without a method word. The handler is no classifier of the signature, because it names a program and not a kind of object. The calculus says what an operator means and which method labels it; the handler realizes the labeled operator under Ρ, the passage from the mathematical operations to their numerical representations ([Ρ](../wiki/rho.md)), written $\mathrm P$ in the formulas below.

Formally, the exact semantics $\Upsilon : \Sigma \to \mathcal B$ assigns each operation symbol its operation in the target algebra $\mathcal B$ of the calculus, fixed on the edges by the interpretation rules of Bellman-SYM's Appendix C and extended to every path by composition. A label denotes nothing under $\Upsilon$: the meaning of a methodized term is $\Upsilon \circ U_m$, the meaning of its erasure (§8), which is the precise form of the statement that the model does not depend on its labels. A label is read by the numerical semantics $\mathrm P$, a functor from the methodized terms, the paths of labeled generators that $M_m$ of §8 produces, into the category $\mathcal D$ of representations, whose value on a labeled generator $(\omega, M, G)$ is a morphism $\mathrm P(\omega, M, G) : \mathrm P X \to \mathrm P Y$ of $\mathcal D$, the routine the handler computes; since a methodized term is a path of labeled generators, $\mathrm P$ is fixed on every one by its values on them. This is the shape of a handler of an algebraic effect in Plotkin and Pretnar (2009): each handler "corresponds to a model of the theory", and handling a term is the homomorphism the model induces (abstract; §3.2 for operation symbols with parameters).[^handlers] Their model satisfies the equations of the effect theory; $\mathrm P$ satisfies the arities of the labeled generators and nothing more, so it is a model of the signature, the theory without its equations. [Functional programming](./APPX-G-FUNCTIONAL-PROGRAMMING.md) §8 writes the realization of a fully specified expression $e$ as $\mathrm P_{c,m,s}(e)$, which is $\mathrm P$ applied to the term $K_s\, M_m\, \mathcal C_c(e)$ with its calibration $c$, its method assignment $m$ and its settings $s$ bound. At the Evaluation of a builder, whose exact value is the identity on the stored function, the realized value under a representation is the idempotent $s \circ r$ of §5 on the function space, whose fixed points are the represented functions; on the stored values it is $r \circ s$, the identity.

Whether the realized operator is close to the exact one, a relation between $\Upsilon$ and $\mathrm P$ composed with the maps that decode a representation, is the semiconjugacy of [Semi-conjugacy](./10-semi-conjugacy.md): a proposition to be proved for each method under its conditions, and no typing fact.

## 8 The two erasures

Two things can be forgotten. Forget the labels: every operator keeps its inputs, outputs and equations, and its method and grid components become empty, while the lines a methods file added, the derivative lines and the rows of a solution method, remain. Forget the methods file: everything it added is removed, and the stage file's own term returns. The first says that the meaning of the operations does not depend on the labels; the second returns the model itself, the stage file.

Formally, the **erasure of the labels** $U_m : \Sigma_m \to \Sigma$ is the functor that is the identity on objects and forgets the parameter of every morphism. A **method assignment** $m$, the entries of a methods file read after the union, defines the methodization $M_m : \Sigma \to \Sigma_m$ on each operation symbol $\omega$ as $\omega$ with the parameter $[m(\omega)]$, and with the identity label on every generator without an entry, so that $M_m$ is total; it extends to every path by composition, the labels concatenated as §3 states, and is a functor because $\Sigma$ is free on its operation symbols. The section law

$$
U_m \circ M_m = \mathrm{id}_{\Sigma}
$$

holds on every path, since forgetting the parameter of $\omega$ with parameter $[m(\omega)]$ returns $\omega$ and a path's parameter is the concatenation of its generators' parameters: $M_m$ is a section and $U_m$ a retraction (Adámek, Herrlich and Strecker 2004, Definitions 7.19 and 7.24, pp. 106–107).[^ahs] $U_m$ is many-to-one, and no isomorphism between a method and anything else is claimed.

The **erasure of the addition** $U_a$ acts on the term before elaboration. A methods file is an addition $A$, and the union $S \sqcup A$ of the stage file $S$ with it keeps the provenance of every item, the file and position it was written at ([addition](../wiki/addition.md)). $U_a$ removes the items whose provenance is $A$, so that

$$
U_a(S \sqcup A) = S .
$$

The union is associative with the empty addition as its unit, and $S$ stands as its leftmost operand, so several additions apply in the order the recipe of the stage's trellis lists them ([specification factory](../wiki/spec_factory.md)) and $U_a$ removes them together. $U_a$ removes what $U_m$ keeps: the derivative lines, the rows of a solution method, the declared settings and any coordinate an addition appends to a signature. On the elaborated stage the result of $U_a$ is the declared level of §9, which the stage stores.

## 9 The two levels of a stage

A solution method computes a builder by lines of its own. The endogenous grid method computes the policy by the Euler equation inverted by the inverse marginal utility, the line of the endogenous grid and the identification of the policy with its candidate,

```bl
[id=policy, policy.method : !egm | policy.grid : !Cartesian(a=(a_min, a_max, n_a))
    | evaluate.method : !Linear] {
    c[>] = 𝑑𝑢𝑖𝑛𝑣(β*D_{a}V[>])       :: [id=InvEuler]
    m[>] = a + c[>]                 :: [id=g_cntn_to_dcsn]
    c[~] = c[>]                     :: [id=cons_choice]
}
```

where `𝑑𝑢𝑖𝑛𝑣` is the inverse of the marginal utility `𝑑𝑢` the stage defines, in place of the maximization `c = argmax_{cp : Γ_c(m)}(𝑢(cp) + β*V[>])` the stage file declares, with `Γ_c(m)` the feasible set of consumption at resources `m`. The stage holds both. The **declared level** is the stage file's own lines, read by `stage.declared`; the **operative level** is the stage as it is computed, in which the **rows** of an addition's block whose method component holds a solution method stand in place of the declared lines of that block. A derivative line, `D_{m}V = 𝑑𝑢(c)`, stands at both levels: it states a property of the value the model defines, the envelope condition, true under the conditions of §10, and defines no new field; the author may write it in the stage file or in the methods file, where derivatives are read, and it adds no step of computation. A solution method that supplies no rows, `!vfi`, leaves the declared lines in place at both levels. Where a block's method component holds no solution method, an appended line is an authored line of the block, and a second definition of a field is refused. The declared level is stored and not reconstructed, so the erasure of §8 returns it exactly. The stage's graphs as elaborated from the stage file, its two initial diagrams and its declared builders ([semgraph](../wiki/semgraph.md)), are unchanged by any binding; what a method changes stands in the operative builders alone, the builders as computed.

The condition under which the method's lines compute the maximization, the first-order condition being necessary and sufficient and the envelope condition holding, is an **obligation**: the stage lists it, under `egm-eligibility`, and checks nothing. Under that condition the operative builder denotes the declared operation; without it the two levels denote different operators. Whether two methods compute the same policy is a separate proposition with its own conditions, and no component of a signature claims it.

## 10 Nothing is differentiated

The language differentiates no expression: no derivative rule is attached to any kind of operation, the derivative operator `D` admits no method, and the only analytic statement of a derivative is a line the author writes. The derivative of a value is read by the **derivative term**, `D_{a}V[>]`, the derivative operator applied to a value field in the coordinate of its subscript ([Fields, functions and types](./04-fields,%20functions%20and%20types.md) §1.2), and it is stated by a **derivative line**, `D_{m}V = 𝑑𝑢(c)`, the envelope condition of the decision value. A row of a method that reads a derivative term declares a reading and nothing more.

Two facts about stored numbers are not differentiation of an expression. At realization, a handler that meets a derivative term no line states evaluates it through the representation the methods file fixed for that value, reading the slope of the stored interpolant, which for piecewise linear interpolation is a step function. Where a value passes through a builder whose lines are identities, expectations or scalings of the value it reads, the realization applies the same linear map to the stored derivative values, so that the envelope line of the decision value serves the stage before it; for an expectation this asserts that the derivative of the expectation is the expectation of the derivative, which holds under a dominating bound. The condition under which a stated derivative line holds, that the value is differentiable and the envelope theorem applies, is an obligation the stage lists under `marginal-reading` and checks nothing; the exchange of the derivative and the expectation is a condition recorded under the name `differentiation-under-the-integral` for the arrival builder it concerns, and no stage lists it yet. Where the law of motion between two perches is not the identity in the differentiated coordinate, the author writes the derivative line with the law's factor, as the buffer-stock arrival builder writes `D_{k}V[<] = E_{ψ,ξ}(R * (Γ*ψ)^(-ρ) * D_{m}V)`, with `Γ` the growth factor of permanent income.

Rischel (2022), "Towards Foundations of Categorical Cybernetics," *Electronic Proceedings in Theoretical Computer Science* 372 (Applied Category Theory 2021), 235–248, Definition 2, p. 236: for a category $\mathcal C$ acted on by a strict monoidal category $\mathcal M$, the objects of $\mathrm{Para}_\bullet(\mathcal C)$ are those of $\mathcal C$, a 1-cell $X \to Y$ is an object $M$ of $\mathcal M$ with a morphism $M \bullet X \to Y$, the composite of $X \to Y$ with parameter $M$ and $Y \to Z$ with parameter $N$ has parameter $N \odot M$, and a 2-cell is a morphism of $\mathcal M$. The trivial action used here, $M \bullet X = X$ with identities for the coherence maps, satisfies the paper's Definition 1 of an actegory (p. 236).
[^jacobs]: B. Jacobs (1999), *Categorical Logic and Type Theory*, Studies in Logic and the Foundations of Mathematics 141, Elsevier, §1.1, pp. 20–21: a family of sets indexed by $I$ is displayed as a map $\varphi : X \to I$ whose fibers over $i$ are the sets of the family, and a fibration is the categorical structure that captures indexing and substitution.
[^carroll]: C. D. Carroll (2006), "The method of endogenous gridpoints for solving dynamic stochastic optimization problems," *Economics Letters* 91(3), 312–320, §2.2, p. 315: "the gridpoints for the policy functions are not predetermined; instead they are endogenously generated from a predetermined grid of values of end-of-period assets."
[^ahs]: J. Adámek, H. Herrlich and G. E. Strecker (2004), *Abstract and Concrete Categories: The Joy of Cats*, free online edition (originally Wiley, 1990), Definition 7.19, p. 106: a morphism $f : A \to B$ is a section provided there is $g : B \to A$ with $g \circ f = \mathrm{id}_A$; Definition 7.24, p. 107: $f$ is a retraction provided there is $g$ with $f \circ g = \mathrm{id}_B$, and $B$ is then a retract of $A$.
[^handlers]: G. Plotkin and M. Pretnar (2009), "Handlers of Algebraic Effects," in *Programming Languages and Systems (ESOP 2009)*, Lecture Notes in Computer Science 5502, 80–94, abstract: "Each such handler corresponds to a model of the theory for the effects at hand. The handling construct […] is interpreted using the homomorphism induced by the universal property of the free model"; §3.2: operation symbols take parameters of base types.
